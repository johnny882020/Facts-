## IDEA 1: Self-serve drawback engine for tariff-era manufacturers/exporters

1. Problem: Manufacturers and importer-exporters pay duty (MFN, Section 301, some 232) on inputs later exported or destroyed, and never file because TFTEA claims need import-to-export matching; the loss falls on the **VP Tax / Director of Global Trade Compliance** at a Fortune 500 industrial/auto-parts/consumer-goods firm.
2. Pool: FACT: ~$1B/yr refunded (GAO 2019, above) - this is the claimed amount, not the unclaimed. Unclaimed estimates: "$3-4B/yr" (drawback specialists, [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/settling-into-tftea-drawback), undated trade press), "$5B" and "$15B" (vendor blogs). Treated as UNVERIFIED; no GAO/CBP unclaimed figure found. Vendor claim, unverified: claims rose from $838M (2017) to $3.9B (2023). INFERENCE: with duty collections far higher post-2025 and 301/232 stacks, the pool is plausibly multiples of $1B, but I cannot size it from a primary source. Sizing basis supported only by the $1B floor.
3. Why now: 2025-26 tariff stack (Sec 232 on autos/parts/steel etc.). CBP ACE validations allow drawback on specific 232 auto/truck-part duties, earliest claim date 1 Nov 2025 ([Ginger briefing, vendor](https://gingercontrol.com/briefings/ace-will-enforce-new-drawback-validations-for-eligible-section-c33b6135)). Eligibility is shifting by proclamation: a vendor briefing says CBP stopped allowing drawback for certain Section 301 forced-labor/Brazil duties effective 12 Aug 2026 ([Ginger](https://gingercontrol.com/briefings/cbp-will-no-longer-allow-drawback-claims-for-section-0896eaef)); other vendor pages conflict (July 2026 cheat sheet said eligible). Unresolved - verify in CSMS. Rule churn is both the pain and the risk.
4. Product: Ingests ACE entry-summary extracts (ACE reports, which importers can self-pull) plus export records (shipper's EEI/AES, ERP) and does 1313(b) substitution matching, tags each duty line by eligibility program, and generates claim files with a per-line eligibility audit trail. First user: trade-compliance manager already paying a broker 15-30% on drawback. Day 1: upload 12 months of ACE entry-summary line data and AES export data; get a ranked recovery estimate by duty line and the missing-document list. Data: ACE reports and ERP exports (CSV) - accessible without long integration; the hard part is the manufacturing/accounting records and the CBP-approved drawback "privilege"/ruling backlog (FreightWaves noted a post-TFTEA privilege application backlog).
5. Why software can win: matching and rule-tracking are mechanical; brokers price on contingency. Incumbents (exist per search): customs brokers C.H. Robinson (publishes drawback guidance, [CHR](https://www.chrobinson.com/en-gb/resources/blog/u-s-duty-drawback)), Livingston International, Alliance CHB (drawback pages); drawback specialists Passport (passportglobal.com blog on drawback), Consyn (substack case studies), Lightsource.ai (drawback guide); trade-content vendor GingerControl. Law firms: not verified by search for drawback specifically; INFERENCE: trade law firms advise but don't run claims.
6. Pricing: vendor-reported contingency 15-30% of recovered ([Ginger guide](https://gingercontrol.com/blog/duty-drawback-service-how-to-choose-provider), vendor), estimator mid-market ~17.5% (vendor). IEEPA/CAPE refund contingency 3-10% (single blog, weak). No independent benchmark.
7. Assumptions and 30-day tests:
 a) Large firms leave material drawback unfiled. Test: pull 10 mid-cap exporters' ACE + AES data (public Census/ImportGenius-type data for import-export overlap) and estimate claimable duty; interview 10 compliance managers on current filing rate.
 b) Eligibility rules are stable enough to automate. Test: read CSMS messages for the last 12 months; log reversals (the 301 reversal above is one). If >2 reversals/quarter, the product needs a rules-subscription, not static logic.
 c) CBP/ACE capacity and privilege approvals don't bottleneck. Test: ask 5 brokers for median time from filing to payment in 2026 (GAO flagged delays in 2019).
8. Confidence: Medium-Low. The pool floor is documented ($1B/yr) but the unclaimed size is UNVERIFIED, the incumbent set is crowded, and rule reversals are live. Strongest piece: rule-change tracking tied to claim files.

## IDEA 2: First-sale valuation evidence vault

1. Problem: Multi-tier sourcing importers pay duty on the middleman's price instead of the lower manufacturer price because upstream invoices and proof of bona fide arm's-length sale are not captured at purchase; loss borne by the **Director of Customs/Import Compliance (or VP Sourcing) at a Fortune 500 apparel, footwear, or consumer retailer**.
2. Pool: FACT: only 8.5% of importers (23,520) and $38.5B (2.4%) of $1.63T imports used first sale in 2008-09 (USITC, above); ITC report said many eligible importers may not use it and could not quantify revenue effect. Vendor claim: 15-30% duty savings in multi-tier chains (vendors; not independent). I did not find a current-year (2024-26) measure; sizing UNVERIFIED beyond the 2009 base. INFERENCE: at 2025 duty rates the dollars per eligible dollar of import value are higher than in 2009.
3. Why now: Tariff stacking raises the value of each dollar of customs value. CBP is sending First Sale Valuation Questionnaires covering entries 1 Jan 2023-31 Dec 2025 (secondary vendor report) - i.e., audit pressure, so documentation, not just claiming, is now the product. Incomplete documentation is the main CBP denial reason (vendor). Sources: [STR Trade on CBP denial](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/str-trade-report/trade-report/october/cbp-denial-of-first-sale-claim-highlights-importance-of-regular-reviews); Moss Adams/Mondaq commentary.
4. Product: Supplier/middleman portal that collects factory invoice, middleman invoice, payment proof, and "who set the price" attestations per PO, links them to entries, and outputs the CBP-ready file. First user: customs compliance manager at a retailer with agent-based sourcing. Day 1: pick top 20 supplier chains by import value; send portal invites; get a go/no-go on first-sale eligibility per chain. Data: PO/invoice PDFs from suppliers (their cooperation is the gating item, not integration).
5. Why a software startup could win: the work is document collection and linking. Incumbents: customs brokers (offer first-sale advisory), consultancies Aprio, Moss Adams, RSM (published first-sale guidance, verified in results), FTZ Consultants, Camto, Customs Support; law firms (Crowell & Moring authored the ITC report alert, Baker Donelson). Software: none found specifically; not exhaustively searched.
6. Pricing: no benchmark found (consultancy fees not located).
7. Assumptions and tests:
 a) Middlemen will share upstream invoices (vendor sources say many refuse). Test: ask 10 importers what share of their top vendors refused.
 b) Eligible-but-unused value is large now. Test: take 5 retailers' entries (public bills of lading) and count chains with agent/trading-company intermediaries.
 c) Claims survive CBP audit. Test: collect 5 case summaries of denials; classify cause (documentation vs. substance). If substance, software cannot fix it.
8. Confidence: Low-Medium. Concept documented by ITC and CBP, but current-size data is missing, legal risk (wrong claims -> penalties), and supplier cooperation is a human bottleneck.

## IDEA 3: Post-IEEPA FTA/USMCA preference "reversion" monitor

1. Problem: USMCA claim rates jumped to ~80% only because it was an exemption from IEEPA duties; with IEEPA tariffs struck down (Feb 2026), the incentive to do origin paperwork falls and eligible goods may revert to paying MFN or paying with no claim; loss falls on the **Head of Global Trade / Tax at a Fortune 500 manufacturer with Mexico/Canada supply chains**.
2. Pool: FACT: see claim-share figures above. $509.7B non-claiming trade in 2024 is NOT a pool: Canada's non-USMCA trade moved mostly duty-free via MFN ([The Logic](https://thelogic.co/news/usmca-canada-usa-exports/)); Flexport official said many skip USMCA when MFN is low. No published estimate of duty paid on USMCA-eligible-but-unclaimed goods; UNVERIFIED. FTA beyond USMCA: only CAFTA-DR 73% (FY2019, non-textile) from CBP. No KORUS measure found. Pool size: UNVERIFIED.
3. Why now: 2025 claim-rate surge then 2026 IEEPA invalidation; USMCA joint review began 1 July 2026 (vendor/CRS items; outcome per one vendor: not renewed at review, agreement remains - unverified). INFERENCE: claim rates may drift back toward 2024 levels (44%) where the leftover pool is the MFN duty on non-zero-rate lines (autos parts, apparel, food) - unmeasured.
4. Product: Compares entry-line data (ACE) against supplier origin certifications and tariff schedules to flag lines paying MFN that likely qualify. First user: compliance analyst. Day 1: upload ACE entry lines; get flagged lines with MFN rate >0 and country of origin MX/CA/KR/CAFTA. Data: ACE reports, supplier origin certificates (the missing piece).
5. Incumbents: customs brokers (C.H. Robinson, Flexport, Livingston), trade software (GingerControl publishes "unclaimed FTA preference audit" content, vendor), Descartes (customsinfo.com), Sphera-type... only the first three verified in results; law firms advise on rules of origin.
6. Pricing: no benchmark found.
7. Assumptions and tests: (a) meaningful MFN duty exists on eligible-unclaimed lines - test: sample 50 lines from public Census data by HS and compute duty at MFN where USMCA rate is 0; (b) claim rates have fallen after Feb 2026 - test: look for a Census/CBP monthly series post-Feb 2026; (c) importers can get supplier certifications - test: ask 10 how long a USMCA certification takes.
8. Confidence: Low. The key quantity (unclaimed duty on eligible goods) is not measured by any source I found; Idea is also retroactive-claim-limited (about 1 year via post-summary correction/protest; not sourced here).

## IDEA 4: Carrier service-guarantee credit recovery for enterprise shippers
1. Problem: Late-delivery refunds from UPS/FedEx are not auto-issued, windows are short (15 days per vendor sources), and exceptions void many; loss borne by **Director of Transportation / Parcel Procurement at a Fortune 500 retailer or distributor**.
2. Pool: only vendor figures ($2,000-$4,000 per small business per year; "millions go unclaimed", [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/wheres-my-parcel-refund-dont-hold-your-breath)). Refund Retriever's own site says ~50% of late shipments do not qualify (vendor). Pool UNVERIFIED. Ground-service refunds were suspended by carriers and not restored per FreightWaves (older article).
3. Why now: nothing found specific to 2024-26. Weak.
4-5. Incumbents are many and verified: Refund Retriever, LateShipment.com, ShipScience, Cost Analysts, ShipRx, Cahoot. Brokers and 3PLs bundle audit.
6. Price: competitor page lists Refund Retriever at 45-50% of refunds for low-spend tiers (competitor claim; unsourced). Contingency 20-50% range is market norm - not independently verified.
7. Assumptions: pool small for Fortune 500 (enterprise contracts often waive guarantees - not sourced). Not worth a 30-day test list; fails sizing.
8. Confidence: Low. **Recommend rejecting** as an enterprise-scale idea: unquantified pool, very crowded, no 2024-26 catalyst.

## REJECTED probes
- **Section 301 ship/port fee refunds:** USTR suspended fees effective 10 Nov 2025 until 9 Nov 2026 ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/11/ustr-port-fee-suspension-what-you-need-to-know)); one report extends relief to 9 Jan 2027 ([Seatrade](https://seatrade-maritime.com/regulations/us-formalises-two-month-suspension-of-chinese-vessel-fees), date unconfirmed). No data found that fees were collected or are refundable -> no pool.
- **VAT/import VAT recovery:** Only vendor figures (EUR 18B/yr, 21% unable to recover cross-border VAT attributed to OECD, $30B global travel-spend) - all UNVERIFIED. Commission has a 2019 PwC refunds study ([EC](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2019-05/vat-refunds-legislative-report.pdf)) but no import-VAT unclaimed number found. Crowded (VAT IT, Taxback, Avalara). Rejected for lack of size evidence; could be revisited by reading the PwC study in full.
- **FTZ savings:** Privileged-foreign status required for tariff-covered goods, which blocks inverted-tariff savings (Rockefeller Group/Miller & Co. via vendor summaries). 1,300 active FTZ operations, $1.1T received+exported, per FTZ Board report via [Air Freight News](https://airfreight.news/articles/full/broward-countys-foreign-trade-zone-no-25secures-top-rankings-in-national-report); no duty-savings figure found. Rejected: savings shrank, not grew.
- **De minimis / Section 321:** An end of an exemption is a new cost, not an unclaimed entitlement. CBP reportedly collected $1B+ since phase-out ([SupplyChain247](https://www.supplychain247.com/article/cbp-collects-1-billion-after-de-minimis-exemption-ends/3pl), secondary). Rejected as off-angle; statutory status (permanent vs repealed July 2027) conflicts across sources.
- **Ocean contract rebates/volume true-ups, cargo insurance claim leakage, fuel tax credits:** no usable sourced size found (ocean search returned shortfall-dispute anecdotes only, e.g. Yang Ming/Dollar General/QVC via The Loadstar snippets; nothing on unclaimed rebates). Not researched deeply for cargo insurance and fuel tax credit (no search spent) - marked UNVERIFIED/unexplored, not rejected on evidence.
