## 1. Rule-by-rule scan (dates, coverage, consequence)

### Takeaway
Seven rules have a live, near-dated, un-diluted obligation inside the window: CBAM (certificates Feb 2027), CBP foreign-importer-of-record rule (Jan 2027), EUDR (Dec 30 2026, but two prior delays), EU Forced Labour Regulation (Dec 14 2027), FSMA 204 (July 20 2028, enforcement barred until then), battery passport (Feb 18 2027, thin), US de minimis (already suspended). Others are already in force, pushed past 2028, or lack a dated action.

### Cited Findings

One secondary source says about 18,000 importers remain in scope; the 18,000 count is the arithmetic implied by 182,000 = 90% and is NOT directly sourced from the Commission — [SDC Exec/other snippet](https://www.sdcexec.com/sustainability/carbon-footprint/news/22954286/sp-global-market-intelligence-cbam-to-add-15b-to-europes-annual-import-bill).

## 2. The three selected ideas

Ranking logic: forced date inside 2027, sourced consequence, thin or beatable competition, and a logistics buyer (importer, broker, forwarder) with budget now.

### IDEA A: CBAM certificate-and-cash workflow for importers and their customs brokers (CBAM "treasury and surrender desk")

1. Rule: certificate sales open 1 Feb 2027; quarterly 50% holding duty from 2027; annual declaration and surrender 30 Sept 2027 for 2026 imports. Who: authorised CBAM declarants importing over 50 t/yr of iron, steel, aluminium, cement, fertiliser, electricity, hydrogen goods. Count: roughly 18,000 in scope is DERIVED (182,000 exempt = 90%), not sourced; 12,000+ applications and about 4,100 authorised as of Jan 2026 per a vendor guide. Penalty: EUR 100/t CO2e (indexed) plus the duty to surrender; unauthorised importers reportedly 3-5x; imports can be blocked at customs (Finnish customs warning: [Tulli](https://tulli.fi/en/-/do-you-import-cbam-goods-make-sure-that-your-imports-are-not-blocked-in-2026)).
2. Spend evidence: the money is the certificate bill, not software. Quarterly price EUR 75.36 (Q1) and 75.28 (Q2) 2026 are Commission-published. Illustrative steel/aluminium bills from vendor guides (about EUR 11,900 and EUR 9,550 per example consignment) are VENDOR illustrations, not sizing. No Commission or audited administrative-cost-per-importer figure found: UNVERIFIED. Total revenue forecasts conflict.
3. Product: A system that ingests customs entries (ACE-equivalent EU declarations, supplier emissions data and default-value fallbacks) and computes live embedded-emission liability per quarter, so finance can pre-buy certificates and reconcile against the 50% holding rule. It produces the declaration and a verifier-ready evidence pack and flags suppliers whose verified data would cut the default-value premium. First user: head of customs or group treasury at a mid-to-large EU steel/aluminium/fertiliser importer, and the customs broker filing for them. Day-1 workflow: upload the last 12 months of customs declarations and supplier data; get 2026 liability estimate and a Q1 2027 holding requirement within a day. Data: customs declarations (CN code, mass, origin), supplier installation emission data, Commission quarterly price, ETS price.
4. Competitors (verified in search results): carbmee (EUR 20M Series A, Dec 2024, CommerzVentures), ARTEM (EUR 1.5M seed, Dec 2024), kolum (EUR 2.1M pre-seed, July 2024), Tanso (EUR 6.5M, date unclear), osapiens, CarbonChain (USD 10M Series A) — [FF News](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/funding/enterprise-carbon-management-company-carbmee-secures-e20-million-series-a-funding-to-accelerate-innovation-and-international-expansion/), [Trending Topics](https://www.trendingtopics.eu/artem-schweizer-cbam-software-erhaelt-15-mio-euro-seed-finanzierung/), [tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2024/07/11/carbon-compliance-platform-kolum-raises-2-1m-pre-seed/), [CarbonChain](https://www.carbonchain.com/blog/carbonchain-raises-10m-series-a-to-accelerate-global-decarbonization-through-carbon-accounting). Crowded on reporting; the certificate-treasury and broker angle looks less served (UNVERIFIED, not systematically checked). SAP/Oracle ERP modules are likely substitutes (UNVERIFIED).
5. Why a startup wins: incumbents sell emissions reporting; the 2027 pain is cash and certificate timing, a finance workflow. Model: per-importer subscription plus broker seats. Benchmark price: none sourced; UNVERIFIED. Key risk: buyers are few (about 4,100 authorised declarants) and many will let brokers or ERP do it.
6. Assumptions and 30-day tests:
   - Mid-size authorised declarants lack a tool for quarterly liability and 50% holding. Test: 15 interviews with declarants and 5 brokers; ask what they do on 1 Feb 2027. Kill if over half say ERP or broker handles it.
   - Default-value fallback makes the bill materially larger than verified data, so there is a savings story. Test: compute 3 real importers' 2026 exposure under default vs supplier data using their entries.
   - Someone will pay before Feb 2027. Test: 5 paid pilots at a stated price or letters of intent.
7. Delay/dilution risk: CBAM has already been diluted once (2025 simplification, 50 t threshold, which cut about 90% of importers). A further Commission proposal to ease or extend (downstream scope) is live in political debate; not verified here. Dates 1 Feb 2027 and 30 Sept 2027 are statutory in an amended regulation; no postponement found in snippets. Moderate risk of further dilution, low risk of repeal.

### IDEA B: Foreign-IOR eligibility and broker-liability desk (CBP IFR, effective 5 Jan 2027)

1. Rule: CBP interim final rule, 91 FR 69,977 (6 Nov 2026), effective 5 Jan 2027; broker verification duty may be delayed to 6 May 2027. Who: every importer of record (must prove it is a "U.S." IOR under ownership-and-assets test), foreign IORs (need CTPAT validation or a CTPAT-validated broker), and licensed customs brokers (expanded liability). Number of affected parties: not found; UNVERIFIED. Consequence: entry restriction for noncompliant foreign IORs; broker liability exposure. CBP rationale cites over USD 1B unpaid principal tied to non-CTPAT foreign importers.
2. Spend evidence: none sourced beyond CBP's USD 1B revenue-loss statement. No regulatory impact analysis read. This is the weakest spend case of the three; flag clearly. The compelling hook is that brokers face liability, which tends to produce tool spend, but that is inference.
3. Product: A verification and onboarding layer for brokers and forwarders that tests each IOR against the rule's U.S./foreign test, collects ownership and asset evidence, checks CTPAT status, and keeps an audit trail per entry. It blocks or routes filings that fail. First user: compliance head at a mid-size customs broker or e-commerce forwarder serving foreign sellers. Day-1 workflow: bulk-upload the IOR list from the broker's client file; get a pass/fail/needs-evidence report in a day. Data: IOR master data, ownership documents, bond data, CTPAT status lookup, entry history.
4. Competitors: no purpose-built product found in searches (UNVERIFIED; I did not search for it specifically). Adjacent: broker platforms (Flexport published guidance on the rule; Flexport is an incumbent forwarder/broker, funding not checked here) and global-trade-management suites. Space looks empty because the rule is 6 days old.
5. Why a startup wins: speed to a brand-new rule; big incumbents' roadmaps lag. Model: per-IOR or per-broker subscription. Benchmark price: none sourced. Key risk: market may be small and broker-owned in-house; CBP may soften the rule after comments.
6. Assumptions and tests:
   - Brokers feel direct liability and will buy rather than update spreadsheets. Test: 10 broker interviews; ask for their Jan 5 plan.
   - Enough IORs fail the ownership/assets test to create work. Test: run the test on 50 anonymised IORs from 2 brokers.
   - CBP will not materially soften the IFR. Test: read the comment docket and read-outs at 30 days; kill if CBP signals a delay.
7. Delay/dilution risk: HIGH relative to others. It is an interim final rule with a comment period ending on the effective date; CBP already gave brokers a delayed verification date (to 6 May 2027). Prior history: Executive-order-driven foreign-IOR measures were narrowed in this rule (informal-entry ban not imposed). Primary text not read.

### IDEA C: FSMA 204 receiving-side traceability exchange for distributors, 3PLs and retailers (date: 20 July 2028)

1. Rule: FDA Food Traceability Rule; enforcement barred until 20 July 2028 by appropriations language. Who: anyone who manufactures, processes, packs or holds foods on the Food Traceability List (produce, shell eggs, soft cheeses, seafood, deli salads, etc.), including distributors, warehouses and retailers. Firm count not retrieved: UNVERIFIED (GAO-24-106563). Penalty: FDA enforcement of FSMA recordkeeping (specific penalties not retrieved). Also 24-hour sortable-spreadsheet requirement on request (from my reading; not re-verified in this run, UNVERIFIED).
2. Spend evidence (best of the three): FDA RIA primary annualized cost about USD 570M/yr (7%), range USD 63M to USD 2.3B, 2020 dollars; extension RIA says delay yields annualized savings of USD 54M to 73M. This is an official government estimate.
3. Product: Ingest suppliers' receiving and shipping events (EDI 856/ASN, GS1 EPCIS where present, PDFs) and convert them to Key Data Elements and Critical Tracking Events, then give the 3PL or retailer a one-click FDA 24-hour sortable file by lot code. First user: food-safety or supply-chain director at a regional produce or foodservice distributor. Day-1 workflow: connect to the WMS/ERP receiving feed and see a gap report: which inbound lots lack traceability lot codes and which suppliers need to send them. Data: ASNs, receiving scans, TLC assignments, supplier contact lists.
4. Competitors: crowded. ReposiTrak (NYSE: TRAK, public), TraceGains plus iFoodDS alliance (March 2026), Nulogy track-and-trace module (August 2026), Starfish, Fusionware; also FoodLogiQ, Trustwell (named, not verified in results; funding not found) — [Food Industry Executive](https://foodindustryexecutive.com/2026/03/tracegains-and-ifoodds-extend-strategic-alliance-to-turn-fsma-204-compliance-into-a-competitive-advantage/), [Perishable News](https://perishablenews.com/?p=433691).
5. Why a startup wins: weakest here. Crowded and 20 months from enforcement; an edge would be a narrow wedge (ingestion of messy supplier data for 3PLs). Model: per-site SaaS. Benchmark price: none sourced.
6. Assumptions and tests:
   - Buyers act before 2028 without enforcement. Test: ask 15 distributors if budget exists in 2027; kill if under 3 say yes.
   - Messy inbound data is the real bottleneck, not recording. Test: sample 5 distributors' ASN files and measure missing lot codes.
   - A narrow wedge can beat bundled suites. Test: 2 design-partner LOIs at a named price.
7. Delay/dilution risk: HIGH, and history is explicit: original 20 Jan 2026, moved 30 months to 20 July 2028 after industry and Congressional pressure; enforcement barred by statute. A further slip or repeal request is plausible.

Runner-up (not selected): EU Forced Labour Regulation (14 Dec 2027). Firm date, no dilution yet, but no cost evidence, risk database not built, only 10 Member States had authorities, and penalties national. A dossier-and-evidence tool pairing EUFLR with UFLPA is plausible; revisit when the Commission database appears.

## 3. Rejected rules and why

- EUDR: date is 7 weeks away and real, Commission cost estimate exists (EUR 8.1B to EUR 2.0B), but two prior delays, downstream DDS filing removed (July 2026), buyers have mostly chosen tools already, and competition is dense (Epoch USD 4.25M seed announced 26 Oct 2026 per ESG Today; Coolset, Resilinc, Tracextech, others).
