## IDEA 1 (strongest): Customs-bond sufficiency and collateral treasury layer

1. Problem / who pays. Importers' continuous customs bonds are re-sized off trailing duties; tariff stacking makes bonds "insufficient" (shipments held, new bond needed) and sureties demand cash or letter-of-credit collateral that sits idle for months. Buyer: Treasurer (collateral/LC lines) co-owned with VP/Director Customs & Trade Compliance at a large importer. INFERENCE: pain is most acute for mid-cap and foreign-IOR importers; Fortune 500 importers often have large surety lines, so the F500 buyer is real but the volume buyer may be the Fortune 1000 / private importer.
2. Size.
 - FACT (secondary): CBP told CNBC it identified 27,479 bond insufficiencies in FY2025 valued at nearly $3.6B, about double the 2019 level. Source type: regulator data via press (Newsquawk relaying CNBC, 2026-02-12). https://newsquawk.com/headlines/us-customs-tells-cnbc-that-for-fiscal-2025-it-has-identified-27-479-customs-bond-insufficiencies-valued-at-nearly-usd-3-6bln-double-the-2019-level-when-insufficiencies-first-soared-due-to-trump-s-first-term-trade-policies-via-cnbc-12-02-2026 (original CNBC and CBP data not opened; outlets round to $3.5B).
 - FACT (secondary): customs duties $195B in FY2025 per the Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget; CBP says $204B incl. taxes/fees from start of administration through FY2025 end. https://www.foxnews.com/politics/cbp-announces-record-breaking-200-billion-tariff-revenue-amid-trump-administration-enforcement-push (news; CRFB figure seen in search summary only).
 - FACT (rule): standard continuous bond = 10% of duties/taxes/fees over rolling 12 months, $50,000 minimum. https://www.trucordia.com/blog/how-tariff-changes-are-driving-up-u.s.-customs-bond-requirements (broker blog; CBP page not opened).
 - FACT (broker quote, weak): collateral generally equals bond amount above $100,000, cash or LC; underwriters may ask LC at 110% of limit. https://spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/us-importers-scramble-bond-outlay-hike-higher-tariffs and https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/export-controls-trade-investment-sanctions/1807590/what-every-multinational-should-know-about-the-new-customs-enforcement-realities-part-i-managing-rising-bond-and-collateral-requirements
 - INFERENCE: if bonds are 10% of duties, then at $195B annual duties the implied bond limits are order of $19.5B (10% formula x $195B); with collateral at 100% for large bonds, tens of billions of idle collateral is plausible but NOT measured. Total market premium or collateral = UNVERIFIED (no SFAA data found).
3. Why now (FACT). Executive Order 14411 "Strengthening Customs Enforcement," 2026-06-03: directs higher minimum bond coverage and minimum domestic assets or bonding for importers of record; bars foreign IORs from continuous bonds; CBP steps due ~90 days (about Sep 1, 2026), DHS IOR overhaul in 180 days (about early Dec 2026). https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260610-new-executive-order-on-strengthening-customs-enforcement-what-importers-need-to-know (law firm; EO text not opened). Surety market tightening: sureties "reacting more aggressively than CBP's formula" (Foley, 2026-06, Mondaq link above).
4. Product. A bond-and-collateral control tower: ingest the importer's ACE/broker entry data to forecast 12-month duty run-rate and predict insufficiency 30-60 days ahead; present a collateral optimization view (cash vs LC vs indemnity vs multi-surety); trigger early bond-increase requests and, if tariffs fall, collateral-release requests. First user: Director of Customs/Trade compliance at a $50M-$1B duties importer, with treasury as budget holder. Day-1: connect ACE reports (importer-granted or via broker), see "days to insufficiency" and collateral-in-place by surety. Data: ACE reports and surety collateral schedules; accessible with importer/broker consent (PMS and ACE statements exist per CBP guidance https://cbp.gov/sites/default/files/assets/documents/2022-Jun/Participating in Periodic Monthly Statements %281%29.pdf); surety terms are not public.
5. Why a startup can win / incumbents. Sureties write the bond and do not optimize the customer's collateral; brokers/agencies sell it. Verified existing: Ginger Control (publishes bond-sufficiency programs and a bond calculator; VENDOR, https://gingercontrol.com/tools/customs-bond-calculator), SuretyOne (surety agency blog, https://suretyone.com/blog/customs-bond-bust-premium-bubbles-and-loss-bubbles-could-be-painful/), Gaia Dynamics (bond content, https://www.gaiadynamics.ai/blog/continuous-customs-bonds-in-2026-why-cbp-keeps-demanding-bigger-bonds), sureties RLI, CNA, Chubb named as customs-bond beneficiaries (https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/ca/news/breaking-news/how-tariffs-are-making-one-sector-boom-for-rli-cna-and-chubb-564578.aspx), Allianz Trade publishes customs-bond guidance. Not verified: whether any of these sells collateral optimization as software. Startup edge: neutral to sureties, owns the forecast.
6. Business model. SaaS $2-6k/month plus a success fee on released collateral (INFERENCE). Benchmark for premium: 0.5-2% of bond amount, S&P cites 1% guideline (broker/press, same links); no benchmark found for software price. Licensing risk: acting as surety broker needs a producer license; offering LC/collateral financing would be a lender (avoid, partner). Do not underwrite.
7. Three assumptions and 30-day tests.
 a. Importers feel the pain at the treasury level (not just a broker headache). Test: 15 calls to treasurers/customs leads with >$20M duties; require at least 5 who can state collateral posted and a bond-increase event in the last 12 months.
 b. Collateral can actually be renegotiated or released when forecasts improve. Test: ask 5 sureties/agents what trigger releases collateral and in what time; kill if answer is "only at bond cancellation plus finalization of entries."
 c. ACE/broker data is obtainable fast. Test: get 3 importers to share one ACE statement export each in a week; reconstruct the 12-month duty run-rate and compare to their current bond.
8. Confidence: Medium-Low. Why: strong regulatory tailwind and a real number (FY2025 insufficiencies), but the primary sources were not opened, collateral market size is UNVERIFIED, and EO rulemaking could change or reverse the tailwind (one analysis says limits would be recalculated down if tariffs fall, https://www.under30ceo.com/tariffs-drive-spike-in-customs-bond-shortfalls/).

## IDEA 2: Shipper-funded carrier quick-pay (dynamic discounting on freight payables)

1. Problem / who pays. Carriers pay 1-3% for quick pay or 1-5% for factoring to get cash in days, while the shipper's own cost of capital is far lower; shippers stretch terms to 45-75 days. Buyer: Treasurer (working-capital lever) and Controller / Director of Transportation Procurement at a shipper.
2. Size.
 - FACT (VENDOR/trade blogs): quick pay typically 1-3% for payment in 1-7 days; factoring 1-5%, most 2-3.5%. https://otrucking.com/resources/guides/freight-factoring-rates-2026/ and https://www.mjnservices.com/blog/is-factoring-worth-it-for-trucking. Conflicting: another source says 2-5% cut. None are regulators.
 - FACT (VENDOR blog, not verified): shippers pushing 45/60/75-day terms; average Transport & Logistics DSO 56 days. https://www.traxtech.com/blog/the-shippers-role-in-the-trucking-cash-flow-crisis. No dated 2025 survey found.
 - FACT: Cass Information Systems processes about $37B of freight payments a year (Oct 2025 investor deck; Q4 2025 transportation volume $9.2B). https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/708781/000070878125000103/investor-presentationxq3.htm (SEC filing, primary).
 - INFERENCE: 2% fee to accelerate about 25 days annualizes to roughly 29% (2% x 365/25). Pool of fees = UNVERIFIED; a 1% to 3% take on a slice of Cass-scale volume would be hundreds of millions, but share of volume eligible is unknown.
3. Why now. Partial: higher rates cost of capital and tariff-driven cash squeeze are asserted in trade press; I found no sourced 2024-2026 change specific to carrier payment terms. Gap.
4. Product. Early-payment offers on approved freight invoices at a shipper-set discount rate, funded by the shipper's balance sheet or a bank partner, with bank-detail verification in the flow. First user: a shipper with a freight payment provider and a carrier-capacity problem. Day-1: carriers see a "get paid in 2 days at x%" button on approved invoices; shipper treasurer sets rate cap. Data: approved-invoice file from the freight payment provider/TMS (accessible by integration, not public).
5. Incumbents. Cass (verified, SEC filing above) is the freight payment incumbent; factoring and quick-pay firms exist (otrucking, MJN, Apex, DAT lists them, VENDOR). I did not verify C2FO, Taulia, PrimeRevenue, or bank SCF programs in this session; treat as likely but UNVERIFIED. Risk: Cass or a bank can bolt this on.
6. Business model. Take 10-25% of the discount spread (INFERENCE). Benchmark: quick pay 1-3% is carrier-paid, VENDOR figures only. Licensing: if you fund, you are a lender (state commercial finance laws); if you move funds, money transmitter exposure; mitigate by bank partner.
7. Assumptions and tests.
 a. Shippers want to spend balance sheet on carrier speed. Test: 10 treasurers; ask if they would fund early pay at SOFR+X on freight; need 3 with a named budget owner.
 b. Carriers prefer this over broker quick pay and factors. Test: survey 50 carriers via a dispatcher community on rate they would accept; look for a median below 1.5%.
 c. Freight payment providers will share invoice data. Test: ask 3 providers for an API/data-share term sheet; kill if all refuse.
8. Confidence: Low. Why: price benchmarks are all VENDOR, no sourced why-now, big incumbent in the payment rail, lender licensing.

## IDEA 3: Duty cash-timing planner (Periodic Monthly Statement, FTZ, bonded warehouse routing)

1. Problem / who pays. Duty cash out is a large, lumpy treasury item; programs that defer it (PMS float, FTZ, bonded warehouse) are used ad hoc. Buyer: Treasurer / Director of Global Trade at an importer.
2. Size.
 - FACT: PMS lets eligible importers pay prior month's duties on the 15th working day of the next month, interest-free. https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/cbp-2026-periodic-monthly-statement-dates/ (broker) and CBP guidance link in Idea 1 (not opened).
 - FACT (conflicting): merchandise received into FTZs about $949B (down $62B from over $1T) vs $963.8B in the 86th FTZ Board report, per secondary coverage; deferral cited as a core benefit by 72% of users, unclear whether that is a user survey. https://siteselection.com/u-s-foreign-trade-zone-rankings-top-zones-help-hedge-risk-move-goods/ . Total duty deferred = UNVERIFIED.
 - FACT: FY2025 duties $195B (CRFB, see Idea 1). INFERENCE: each $1B of duties deferred 30 days is worth about $1B x r x 30/365; at an assumed r (not sourced) of 5% that is about $4M. The rate is an assumption, so treat as illustrative only.
3. Why now. Duties more than 2.5x FY2024 (CRFB via search summary), so float per entry is larger. No sourced new deferral rule found.
4. Product. Models which entries belong in PMS, FTZ admission or bonded warehouse given duty rate, dwell time, and drawback, and quantifies cash and interest benefit. First user: importer with an existing PMS but no FTZ. Day-1: upload 12 months of entries, get a ranked list of lanes where deferral saves most. Data: entry summaries, accessible from broker/ACE.
5. Incumbents. Customs brokers, trade-compliance suites (Ginger Control publishes landed-cost/duty content, VENDOR); FTZ operators and consultants (NAFTZ, FTZ consultants exist per results). Not verified who sells an optimizer.
6. Business model. SaaS or savings share (benchmark: no benchmark found). No licensing for the software; FTZ operation needs Board approval (not pursued).
7. Assumptions and tests.
 a. Most big importers have not already captured the float. Test: ask 10 customs leads if all eligible entries are on PMS; kill if 8 say yes.
 b. Savings exceed software price. Test: model 3 customers' entries; need over $250k/yr identified.
 c. Data access in days. Test: 3 ACE exports in 2 weeks.
8. Confidence: Low. Why: float is small once PMS is adopted, rate not sourced, likely a feature of existing trade-compliance tools.

## IDEA 4: Deep-tier (tier-2/3) supplier finance underwritten from tier-1 purchase data

1. Problem / who pays. Buyer-led SCF stops at tier-1; deep-tier suppliers cannot access cheap credit. Buyer: Treasurer or Chief Procurement Officer at a manufacturer, with a bank funding.
2. Size.
 - FACT: ADB 2025 Trade Finance Gap Survey puts the gap at $2.5T, unchanged from 2023, about 10% of merchandise trade; 110+ providers surveyed; SME rejection rate 41%. https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2026/01/15/global-trade-finance-gap-at-25-trillion-as-global-trade-tensions-rise-adb-says (press on an MDB survey; ADB original not opened). Critic notes it is survey-based, not measured.
 - Share of the gap that is tier-2/3 = UNVERIFIED (not found).
3. Why now. ADB points to supply chain finance for lower-tier SMEs and digitalization as levers; 80% of banks expect demand growth. Same link.
4. Product. Score tier-2/3 suppliers using the tier-1's PO and payment data plus customs data, then offer a bank-funded pre-shipment or invoice finance line. First user: a procurement/treasury team of a manufacturer with tier-1 suppliers willing to share sub-supplier data. Data accessibility: low; visibility is cited as the main barrier (ERP ends at tier 1), GTR/Procurious/vendor sources.
5. Incumbents. Banks and SCF vendors exist; verified deep-tier offerings in results: FCI Reverse (fci.nl), Vayana (VENDOR), Credable (VENDOR), ICC Academy writes on deep-tier. Coverage figures not found.
6. Business model. Bank-funded origination fee; benchmark: no benchmark found. Licensing: lender or bank partnership; KYC/AML for many small sub-suppliers is cited as costly.
7. Assumptions and tests.
 a. Tier-1 suppliers will disclose their suppliers. Test: ask 10 tier-1s; kill if under 3 agree.
 b. Tier-1 purchase data predicts tier-2 credit risk. Test: back-test on one dataset if any bank provides default data.
 c. A bank will fund on that scoring. Test: 5 bank trade-finance heads; need one pilot LOI.
8. Confidence: Low. Why: real macro gap but crowded, data-blocked, regulated, and no sizing of the tier-2/3 slice.

## REJECTED

- Freight payment bank-detail/payee verification. Demand is real: FBI IC3 2025 BEC losses $3,046,598,558 from 24,768 complaints (https://nationalcioreview.com/articles-insights/extra-bytes/fbi-cybercrime-report-reveals-20-8-billion-lost-in-2025/ , summary of IC3 report; PDF at ic3.gov); AFP 2026 survey: 74% of organizations hit by BEC in 2025 vs 63% in 2024, though Digital Transactions reports 70% (https://www.financialprofessionals.org/about/learn-more/press-releases/Details/over-75-percent-of-us-firms-experienced-payments-fraud-in-2025-while-ai-adoption-for-fraud-mitigation-lags); Nacha Phase 2 fraud-monitoring rule effective around June 19-22, 2026 (https://www.nacha.org/rules/risk-management-topics-fraud-monitoring-phase-2); Truckstop VENDOR figure $455M 2024 freight fraud losses (https://www.trucknews.com/transportation/freight-fraud-losses-surpass-455m-in-2024). Rejected because crowded and bundled: Trustpair (ERP-integrated bank-account validation, 190 countries, Coupa/SAP; VENDOR; https://trustpair.com/account-validation-compliance/), Highway (serves 1,050+ brokers, FTV Capital growth investment Aug 2025), GenLogs ($60M Series B), Indemni ($4M seed), FMCSA IDEMIA identity proofing for new registrants since April 2025. Also the freight-specific fraud number is VENDOR and not a clean TAM, and the freight payer incumbent Cass sits in the payment rail.

Exists: Otonomi (3-hour air cargo delay, maritime, trucking), CargoAI CargoSHIELD+, Apollo/ZenHedge (tender-rejection freight-cost cover, Jan 2026), Tag-N-Trac/Solent TRACinSURED. - Carmack/COGSA subrogation recovery. Mechanics confirmed (strict carrier liability; 9-month claim and 2-year suit windows often; COGSA $500/package cap shrinks recoveries; Marshall Dennehey, Mondaq, FreightWaves search results), but I found no annual claim dollar figure, no recovery-rate benchmark, and the BTS report snippet had no numbers. Cannot be sized; rejected for now. Next step would be the BTS/DOT loss-and-damage report PDF.
- Landed-cost accuracy / margin distortion. No primary survey: the 5-12% duty-calc error range is attributed to a Thomson Reuters 2024 survey only via a Ginger Control blog (VENDOR, unconfirmed); 8-25% and 20-30% underestimation claims are unsourced blogs. 2026 TR report stats (72% name tariff volatility top risk, 7% use dedicated software; 225 respondents) also second-hand. Crowded with duty calculators (Avalara, Ginger). Rejected.
- In-transit inventory cash / in-transit title financing. Searched for BIS/Fed/OECD data on inventory-in-transit working capital; got only vendor blogs with hypothetical examples. No sizing available. Rejected pending data (Census or Fed inventory-in-transit series not located).
