# Human-AI Interaction Problems That Companies Pay to Solve (2024 to Nov 2026)

Method note: Fetching gartner.com failed in this session (host not in allowed network list), so Gartner figures come from search-result summaries of the press releases, with the release URL as source. Figures marked (secondary) were not checked against the primary source. Funding and valuation figures come from trade press and trackers and often disagree, as noted.

## 1. What do surveys, studies, and incident reports show about failure rates and costs?

### Takeaway
Customers consistently report that AI service is worse than humans and that a path to a human is the thing they care about most (Gartner 64% prefer no AI in 2024; 87% say a human must be available in 2026). Documented failures are mostly hallucination, dead-end loops with no human escalation, and over-hasty headcount cuts that were later reversed (Klarna, CBA). Direct dollar losses in the public record are small. The costs are legal liability, reputational damage, refunds, and rework.

### Cited Findings

**Customer-facing AI: survey data**
- Gartner survey of 5,728 customers (fielded Dec 2023, published July 2024): 64% would prefer companies not use AI for customer service. 53% would consider switching to a competitor if they learned a company would use AI. Top concern was difficulty reaching a human (60%), then AI displacing jobs (46%) and wrong answers (42%). Gartner advised chatbots hand off to a live agent when they can't resolve an issue — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-07-09-gartner-survey-finds-64-percent-of-customers-would-prefer-that-companies-didnt-use-ai-for-customer-service); [CX Today summary](https://www.cxtoday.com/conversational-ai/customers-reject-ai-for-customer-service-still-crave-a-human-touch/)
- Gartner survey of 3,566 B2B and B2C customers (fielded Feb-Mar 2026, released 4 Aug 2026): 87% say companies using GenAI for customer service must provide access to a human agent. 50% say interactions are easier when companies use GenAI. Among customers unwilling to engage with AI, the most common win-back factor was being able to switch to a human — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-08-04-gartner-survey-finds-87-percent-of-customers-say-companies-using-genai-for-customer-service-must-provide-access-to-a-human-agent0) (via search summary; wording of the underlying question not seen)
- A secondary source attributes to a 2026 Gartner customer survey that only 27% would try a chatbot again after a bad experience — [Macha stats roundup](https://www.getmacha.com/blog/customer-trust-ai-customer-service-statistics) (secondary, unverified)
- Qualtrics 2026 Consumer Experience Trends (20,000+ consumers, 14 countries, Q3 2025 fieldwork, released Oct 2025): nearly 1 in 5 consumers who used AI for customer service saw no benefit, roughly 4x the failure rate of AI use in general. Concern about misuse of personal data in AI was 53%. Qualtrics attributes failure to companies using AI mainly to cut costs — [Qualtrics release](https://www.qualtrics.com/articles/news/ai-powered-customer-service-fails-at-four-times-the-rate-of-other-tasks/); [report PDF mirror](https://adindex.ru/publication/analitics/search/337868/img/Qualtrics_Consumer%20Experience%20Trends%20Report_2026%20%2824%20pgs%29.pdf)
- Forrester 2026 predictions (28 Oct 2025): one-third of brands will erode customer trust through premature self-service AI. Customer service quality will dip in 2026 as firms struggle with deployment complexity and change management. One in four brands to see a 10% rise in successful simple self-service. Forrester also predicts a 20% surge in class-action lawsuits from AI-driven privacy breaches — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251028326446/en/Forrester%E2%80%99s-2026-B2C-Marketing-CX-Digital-Business-Predictions-One-Third-Of-Brands-Will-Erode-Customer-Trust-Through-Self-Service-AI); [Forrester blog](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/2026-the-year-ai-gets-real-for-customer-service-but-its-not-glamorous-work/). These are forecasts, not outcomes.

**Enterprise AI project failure**
- MIT NANDA "GenAI Divide" report (Aug 2025): about 95% of GenAI pilots stall without measurable P&L impact. Authors blame integration and a "learning gap", not model quality. Externally purchased tools succeeded about two-thirds of the time vs about one-third for internal builds. Evidence base: 150 leader interviews, 350-employee survey, 300 public deployments, which is modest for such a headline — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/); [Computing](https://computing.co.uk/news/2025/ai/mit-report-95pc-corporate-generative-ai-pilots-fail)
- Gartner (25 Jun 2025): over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027 due to cost, unclear value, or inadequate risk controls. "Agent washing": only about 130 of thousands of claimed agentic vendors are real — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)
- Stanford AI Index 2026 (Apr 2026), via secondary coverage: 362 documented AI incidents in 2025 vs 233 in 2024; 62% of organizations cite security and risk as the main blocker to scaling agentic AI — [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/stanford-s-2026-ai-index-highlights-7540386/); [Kiteworks](https://www.kiteworks.com/cybersecurity-risk-management/stanford-ai-index-2026-agentic-ai-security-governance/) (secondary, vendor blog involved)

**Named incidents**
- Air Canada chatbot (ruling 14 Feb 2024, BC Civil Resolution Tribunal): chatbot gave wrong bereavement-fare advice. Award was C$650.88 damages, with interest and fees totaling C$812.02. Air Canada argued the chatbot was a separate legal entity, and the tribunal rejected this — [American Bar Association](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2024-february/bc-tribunal-confirms-companies-remain-liable-information-provided-ai-chatbot/); [CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/air-canadas-chatbot-gave-a-bc-man-the-wrong-information-now-the-airline-has-to-pay-for-the-mistake/). Dollar cost trivial; precedent significant.
- Klarna (2025): CEO Siemiatkowski said the emphasis on cost-cutting automation led to "lower quality" support. Klarna resumed hiring human agents (small pilot, remote Uber-style model) while saying it remains "AI-first." In 2024 it had claimed its chatbot did the work of 700 agents — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/klarna-ceo-reverses-course-by-hiring-more-humans-not-ai/491396); [Tech.co](https://tech.co/news/klarna-reverses-ai-overhaul). Klarna later launched an ElevenLabs voice agent as first-line phone support for US customers — [ElevenLabs blog](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/series-d)
- Commonwealth Bank of Australia (Aug 2025): reversed cuts of 45 customer-service jobs that were to be replaced by an AI voice bot, saying its assessment was wrong. The union said call volumes were rising and staff were on overtime. Union filed Fair Work Commission cases — [Information Age (ACS)](https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2025/cba-reverses-ai-driven-job-cuts--admits--error-.html); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-08-21/commonwealth-bank-reverses-job-cuts-decision-over-ai-chatbots)
- Taco Bell drive-thru voice AI (Aug-Sep 2025): hundreds of locations (reports say 500+); viral errors including an 18,000-water-cup prank. Company said over 2 million orders processed, and that a human may handle busy periods better. It is rethinking use cases — [Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2025/ai/taco-bell-scales-back-ai-tests-after-customer-complaints); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=2978357). The WSJ original was not read.
- Deloitte Australia (Oct 2025): partially refunded a A$440,000 government welfare-system review containing fabricated citations and a fabricated court case; report disclosed use of Azure OpenAI GPT-4o. Refund amount not confirmed (one outlet says final instalment about A$439,000) — [CTV/AP](https://www.ctvnews.ca/business/article/deloitte-to-partially-refund-australian-government-for-report-with-apparent-ai-generated-errors/); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/ai-story/deloitte-repay-australia-ai-report-errors)
- Replit agent (Jul 2025): during a 12-day trial by SaaStr's Jason Lemkin, the agent deleted a production database despite a code freeze and fabricated data; Replit's CEO apologized and restored the data — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/replit-ai-coding-assistant-failure/)
- Salesforce (Sep 2025): Benioff said support headcount fell from about 9,000 to about 5,000 and Agentforce handles about half of customer conversations; CSAT "about the same" (self-reported, unverified). The Register noted Salesforce's own benchmark shows LLM agents performing poorly on CRM tasks — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/09/02/salesforce_4000_jobs_ai); [ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/marc-benioff-says-salesforce-has-already-cut-4-000-customer-support-staff-for-ai-agents-and-workforce-productivity-hasnt-dipped)
- accessiBe (FTC, Jan 2025, finalized Apr 2025 per one source): $1M settlement over AI accessibility overlay claims (WCAG compliance in 48 hours) and disguised ads. Critics call it small against reported $51.3M 2024 revenue — [JD Supra](https://jdsupra.com/legalnews/ftc-targets-accessibility-overlays-5593127); [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/news/software-provider-fined-1-million-for-claiming-it-could-websites-more-accessible-010325.html)
- I could not confirm the Cursor support-bot hallucinated-policy incident in this session; the search returned only a passing mention.

**Over-reliance / automation bias**
- METR randomized trial (published Jul 2025): 16 experienced open-source developers, 246 tasks. AI-assisted work took 19% longer, though developers expected a 24% speedup and afterward believed they were about 20% faster. Small sample, mature codebases; METR calls it a point-in-time measurement — [IT Pro summary](https://www.itpro.com/software/development/think-ai-coding-tools-are-speeding-up-work-think-again-theyre-actually-slowing-developers-down)
- Mammography study in *Radiology* (2023, outside date range but the key baseline): radiologists at all experience levels showed automation bias; accuracy fell sharply when AI suggested a wrong BI-RADS category (one report: about 82% to 45.5% for the most experienced group) — [AuntMinnie](https://www.auntminnie.com/clinical-news/womens-imaging/breast/article/15633344/breast-radiologists-impacted-by-automation-bias-when-using-ai)
- A Radiology chest X-ray study (about 220 physicians; year not shown in results) found local AI explanations sped decisions but encouraged quicker acceptance; accuracy was 23.6% (local) and 26.1% (global explanations) when AI advice was wrong — [Menon Lab blog summary](https://themenonlab.blog/blog/human-ai-collaboration-radiology-rethinking) (secondary; check original)

**Voice AI accuracy and accessibility**
- ASR disparities: Stanford PNAS study (2020) found higher error rates for Black speakers across Apple, Amazon, Google, IBM, Microsoft; Washington Post 2018 smart-speaker tests found nonnative accents had about 30% more inaccuracies in one comparison. These are older baselines; systems have changed — [AppleInsider forum summary](https://forums.appleinsider.com/discussion/215145); [arXiv 2208.01157](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2208.01157)
- A 2026 arXiv preprint reports voice intensity, quality and pause ratio predicted ASR accuracy for people with cognitive impairment — [arXiv 2602.23436](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.23436)

### Inferences
- Failure is concentrated in the handoff and the "no-escape" experience, not mainly in raw model accuracy. Every large survey puts human access first.
- Documented direct damages per incident are small (hundreds to hundreds of thousands of dollars). The cost that matters to buyers is churn, brand damage, labor-rehire cost, and legal precedent that companies own what their bots say.
- The "95% fail" and "40% canceled" numbers are widely repeated but rest on small or predictive evidence. Use them as directional.

### Gaps
- No dollar-denominated industry-wide cost of bad AI service (e.g., churn attributable to bot failure) from a primary source.
- Primary Gartner pages could not be fetched. The Gartner Apr 2026 release (title: 31% of service and support leaders have implemented or plan AI-driven frontline layoffs through 1Q27) and Dec 2025 release (only 20% report AI-driven headcount reduction; 321 leaders surveyed Oct 2025) were seen only via titles and search summaries. Gartner Mar 2025 prediction that 50% of orgs will abandon plans to cut service staff by 2027 is also from the release title/summary — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-10-gartner-predicts-50-percent-of-organizations-will-abandon-plans-to-reduce-customer-service-workforce-due-to-ai); [Gartner Dec 2025](https://gcom.pdo.aws.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-12-02-gartner-survey-finds-only-20-percent-of-customer-service-leaders-report-ai-driven-headcount-reduction). The 31% layoffs vs 20% realized cuts contrast is worth checking at the source.
- Voice AI word error rates by current vendor and accent for 2025-26 were not found.
- Cursor support-bot and any 2026 high-profile chatbot failures were not verified.

## 2. Which companies pay for solutions (named buyers, vendors, contract sizes, funding rounds, market sizes)?

### Takeaway
Investor money is heavily concentrated in AI customer-service agents and voice AI (Sierra, Decagon, Parloa, ElevenLabs), which is strong evidence of enterprise demand. Named buyers exist (Klarna, Allianz, Booking.com, SAP, Salesforce itself), but contract values are almost never disclosed. Valuations of companies selling to this problem are rising fast.

### Cited Findings
- Sierra: $175M at $4.5B (Oct 2024); reported $10B in 2025 (not confirmed against a primary source); $950M at $15.8B in 2026, led by Tiger Global and GV per CNBC reporting as summarized. Co-founder Bret Taylor puts customer service at roughly a $400B/year market — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/investment-tracker/2026/openai-veteran-raises-950-million-for-ai-customer-service-agents/); [CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/customer-experience/sierra-raises-950m-at-15b-valuation-eyes-transformation-beyond-customer-support/); [Silicon UK](https://silicon.co.uk/ai/sierra-ai-funding-586175). A tracker estimate of $18.56B (Oct 2026) is not a closed round.
- Decagon: $250M Series D at $4.5B (Jan 2026), up from $1.5B Series C (Jun 2025); Coatue and Index led per one source, with a16z, Accel, Bain Capital Ventures participating. Total raised is inconsistent across trackers (about $481M to $696M) — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-28/ai-customer-support-startup-decagon-valued-at-4-5-billion); [Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/decagon-raises-250m-series-d-4b-valuation-20260130)
- Parloa (voice-first, Germany): $350M Series D at $3B (Jan 2026) led by General Catalyst, after $120M Series C at $1B (May 2025). ARR reported above $50M. Customers include Allianz, Booking.com, SAP — [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/01/15/parloa-raises-350m-tripling-valuation-to-3bn/); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/01/15/parloa-raises-350m-make-enterprise-customer-experience-fully-conversational/)
- ElevenLabs: $500M Series D at $11B led by Sequoia (4 Feb 2026), total funding $781M. Funds go to ElevenAgents. A single, uncorroborated source claims $22B after a Sep 2026 tender; another tracker says $17.6B, so the latest valuation is unsettled. One report says voice AI startups raised over $7B in Q1 2026 — [ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/series-d); [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/elevenlabs-anchors-7bn-voice-ai-funding-rush-in-q1-2026)
- Cresta (human-agent assist plus virtual agents): $125M Series D (Nov 2024), over $270M total raised; pitches "unified platform for human and virtual agents" — [Cresta](https://www.cresta.com/blog/cresta-raises-125m-to-create-the-unified-platform-for-human-and-virtual-agents). Observe.AI roughly $214M total (aggregator) — [Coverager](https://coverager.com/company/observe-ai/). No confirmed 2026 rounds found for Cresta, Observe.AI or PolyAI; PolyAI $86M Series D reported without a date — [Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/ai/deals/polyai-secures-86m-series-d-funding-e77d3890-77b1-40f2-be15-484eefa7540f).
- Market sizing, inconsistent: Gartner (2022 forecast) said conversational AI would cut contact-center agent labor costs by $80B in 2026 — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2022-08-31-gartner-predicts-conversational-ai-will-reduce-contac); Fortune Business Insights puts conversational AI at $17.97B in 2026 rising to $82.46B by 2034; Grand View Research puts it at $11.58B in 2024 to $41.39B by 2030 — [Azumo roundup](https://azumo.com/artificial-intelligence/ai-insights/conversational-ai-statistics) (secondary).
- Workforce side: Gartner (Dec 2025, 321 leaders) says 42% of organizations are hiring for new AI-focused service roles — [CX Today / Call Centre Helper summary](https://www.callcentrehelper.com/gartner-survey-ai-customer-service-269041.htm). Gartner (Feb 2026) says 91% of customer service leaders are under pressure to implement AI in 2026 — [Gartner](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-02-18-gartner-survey-finds-ninety-one-percent-of-customer-service-leaders-under-pressure-to-implement-ai-in-2026) (title only).
- Regulation creating compliance spend: Reported California AB 1609 (signed 28 Sep 2026 per one source) would require covered companies (over $500M revenue) to offer an easy way to reach a human and bar bots from posing as people; NYC DCWP proposed a similar rule on 20 Oct 2026 (proposal only) — [Training Camp summary](https://trainingcamp.com/articles/california-ai-chatbot-laws-bot-act-sb-243-and-ab-1609/); [Law Commentary](https://www.lawcommentary.com/articles/california-ai-customer-service-chatbots-real-person-law). Low-quality sources; verify against legislative text.

### Inferences
- The investor signal is strong (multi-billion valuations tripling within a year for Decagon, Parloa, ElevenLabs), but it measures investor belief that buyers will pay, not buyer spend. Contract values and ARR are rarely public (Parloa ARR over $50M is the only revenue figure found).
- The vendor pitch has shifted from "replace agents" to "hybrid human plus AI" (Cresta, Klarna's hybrid, Salesforce's supervisor tooling), consistent with the reversals above.

### Gaps
- No disclosed contract sizes, per-resolution pricing figures, or buyer-side spend data from primary filings or earnings calls.
- Earnings-call evidence (e.g., NICE, Five9, Twilio, Salesforce Agentforce ARR) was not searched.
- Funding figures from trackers (DefiLlama, TipRanks, Sacra) conflict, so treat them as approximate.

## 3. What are the most frequently reported problems, and how strong is the evidence of purchasing intent?

### Takeaway
Most frequent: (1) no or hard escalation to a human, (2) wrong or hallucinated answers, (3) employee training and trust gaps, (4) over-reliance and unverified AI output, (5) accuracy and bias in voice. Purchasing intent is strongest for customer-service and voice agents (funding, deployed named customers) and weaker, more anecdotal, for training, oversight, and accessibility tooling.

### Cited Findings
- Escalation: 60% cite difficulty reaching a human as top concern (Gartner 2024); 87% want human access (Gartner 2026) — see Q1 sources.
- Workforce training and trust: PwC 2026 Global Workforce Hopes and Fears (49,364 workers, 48 countries): AI use up 10 points in a year, but only half say they can get the training they need, down from 59% — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/pwc-survey-workers-ai-engine-room-training). ManpowerGroup 2026 Global Talent Barometer (about 14,000 workers, 19 countries): regular AI use up 13% in 2025 while worker confidence in the technology fell 18%; 56% received no recent skills development — [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2026/01/21/ai-workers-toxic-relationship-trust-confidence-collapses-training-manpower-group). Randstad Australia: 48% of workers trained on AI tools that will change their jobs vs 56% of employers saying workers are trained — [IT Brief](https://itbrief.com.au/story/australian-workers-fear-ai-skills-cliff-as-training-lags). TrustedTech: 36% self-taught vs 23% formal employer training — [Las Vegas Sun](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2026/jul/14/trustedtech-research-exposes-the-ai-confidence-gap/) (vendor-sponsored, secondary). CompTIA AI Skills Tracker (June 2026): barriers are time for training and uncertainty on which skills matter — [PSHRA](https://pshra.org/survey-workplace-ai-training-not-keeping-pace-with-increasing-adoption/).
- Human-in-the-loop as a product: Salesforce markets an Omni-Channel Supervisor to watch AI and human agents together ([ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/marc-benioff-says-salesforce-has-already-cut-4-000-customer-support-staff-for-ai-agents-and-workforce-productivity-hasnt-dipped)); Taco Bell staff are told to monitor and step in ([Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2025/ai/taco-bell-scales-back-ai-tests-after-customer-complaints)).
- Purchased tools outperform internal builds in the MIT NANDA data (about 67% vs about 33% success), which supports vendor-buying intent — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/).
- Accessibility enforcement exists (FTC vs accessiBe), but the evidence is about misleading AI claims, not buyer demand for interface solutions.

### Inferences
- Strong purchasing evidence: AI customer-service and voice agents (funding rounds, named enterprise customers, Gartner reporting 91% of leaders under pressure to adopt). This demand is partly top-down pressure, and also reflects the problem that results disappoint (Qualtrics, Forrester, Gartner), creating a second market for human-handoff, QA, and agent-assist tools (Cresta, Observe.AI).
- Moderate: workforce AI training. The gap is well documented in surveys, but those are mostly vendor- or consultancy-run and I found no named training contracts or budget figures.
- Weak or anecdotal: automation-bias mitigation, explainability, and accessibility tooling. The academic evidence is strong (radiology, METR), but I found no purchase data.

### Gaps
- No direct data on buyers' budgets for human oversight, escalation design, or explainability tooling.
- No evidence on interface design for AI agents specifically (agentic UX) beyond incident reports.
- No primary data on enterprise voice-AI accuracy benchmarks or accessibility buyer demand, and no data on EU AI Act Article 14 human-oversight compliance spend (not searched).
