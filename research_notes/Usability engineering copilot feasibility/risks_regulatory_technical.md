## 1. Is the tool a QMS tool that needs validation (FDA CSA / ISO 13485 4.1.6)? What validation evidence do buyers expect?

### Takeaway
Yes, most likely. A tool whose output becomes part of the usability engineering file or risk management file is software used in the quality system, so buyers must validate it under ISO 13485 4.1.6 (EU and, since QMSR, US) using FDA's risk-based Computer Software Assurance (CSA) approach. The buyer carries the validation obligation, but a vendor who ships a ready-made validation package (intended use, risk assessment, test evidence, change/version control) removes a major sales objection.

### Cited Findings
- FDA issued final guidance "Computer Software Assurance for Production and Quality System Software" on 24 Sept 2025 (CDRH/CBER), finalizing the Sept 2022 draft. It supplements the 2002 General Principles of Software Validation and supersedes GPSV Section 6. It keeps a risk-based, least-burdensome approach and is non-binding (US) — [NSF](https://www.nsf.org/life-science-regulatory-news/fda-final-guidance-computer-software-assurance-csa); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/fda-finalizes-computer-software-assurance-guidance-for-production-and-quality-system-software); [Federal Register 2025-18468](https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents/2025-18468.json)
- **Update:** FDA's page now lists a version titled "Computer Software Assurance for Production and Quality *Management* System Software" (3 Feb 2026). It states that it supersedes the 24 Sept 2025 final guidance, apparently to align with the QMSR (US). Cite the Feb 2026 version as current — [FDA guidance page](https://fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/computer-software-assurance-production-and-quality-management-system-software) (seen via search result, not fetched); [Complizen (vendor) on QMSR AI tool validation](https://www.complizen.ai/post/ai-tool-validation-under-the-qmsr-how-to-validate-ai-tools-and-keep-audit-trails)
- One third-party summary says the final CSA text makes clear it can apply to AI tools used in production/quality systems. This could not be confirmed in the FDA text — [gmpinsiders](https://gmpinsiders.com/?p=9021) (treat as unverified)
- Johner Institute: LLM-based systems count as computerized systems. When used in QM processes they fall under ISO 13485, and 4.1.6 has no stated exception. An exception can be argued only if every output is subsequently fully verified (EU/international) — [Johner Institute, "Validating ChatGPT"](https://blog.johner-institute.com/regulatory-affairs/validating-chatgpt/) (via search summary)
- VDE (German): QMS software must be validated with a risk-based approach before first use and after changes to the software or its application. Under the QMSR, the old 21 CFR 820.70(i) text no longer stands alone, and ISO 13485 clauses govern — [VDE, Validierung von QMS-Software](https://www.vde.com/topics-de/health/beratung/validierung-von-qms-software)
- ISO 13485 4.1.6 validation effort "shall be proportionate to the risk associated with the use of the software"; scope test is whether output affects a QMS process, decision or record — [Complizen (vendor)](https://www.complizen.ai/post/does-your-medical-device-company-need-an-ai-use-policy-what-iso-13485-actually-requires); [Hardian Health](https://www.hardianhealth.com/insights/computer-software-validation-ai)
- A PMDA (Japan) document indicates 4.1.6 covers QMS software beyond the specific 7.5.6/7.6 requirements. Some vendor content wrongly cites clause 7.6 for software validation — [PMDA](https://www.pmda.go.jp/files/000236290.pdf); [NovaQMS](https://novaqms.pages.dev/blog/why-chatgpt-is-not-safe-for-quality-management-records/)
- Practitioner pattern for validating LLMs in GxP: define scope/intended use, build evidence, prepare an auditor checklist — [DEV Community practitioner post](https://dev.to/priya_nair_ree/how-i-validate-llms-for-gxp-work-scope-evidence-and-the-auditors-checklist-1jf) (low authority)

### Inferences
- Validation burden scales with intended use. If the tool is positioned as a drafting assistant whose every output is reviewed, verified and approved by a qualified human under the buyer's document-control SOP, CSA lets buyers do lighter "unscripted/ad-hoc" assurance. If it is positioned as producing records directly (e.g., auto-populating the use-related risk analysis), higher-rigor scripted testing applies.
- Non-determinism and silent model updates from the LLM provider are the hardest validation problem. Buyers will expect pinned model versions, change notifications, regression test suites with a "golden set" of usability/risk examples, and audit trails (prompt, model version, output, reviewer, approval). This list is inferred from the CSA "before first use and after changes" principle above; no vendor-specific buyer survey was found.
- A vendor "validation accelerator pack" (intended-use statement, vendor risk assessment, test protocols and reports per release, traceability, release notes, SOC 2 report) is the likely expected deliverable, by analogy with eQMS vendors. No source quantified this.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch FDA's Feb 2026 CSA text to confirm any explicit AI language or vendor-evidence language.
- No survey found of what medtech buyers actually demand from AI-tool vendors in supplier qualification (ISO 13485 7.4 purchasing controls).

## 2. FDA and notified body stance on AI-generated regulatory content

### Takeaway
No regulator prohibits AI-drafted submissions or technical documentation, and none requires disclosure for device submissions (no such requirement found). Accountability stays wholly with the manufacturer. FDA itself now uses generative and agentic AI (Elsa) to review submissions, and Elsa's own hallucination problems show why reviewer verification is essential. Notified bodies judge documentation on whether it shows GSPR compliance, not on who or what drafted it. Generic, boilerplate AI text risks failing that test.

### Cited Findings
- FDA launched Elsa, an agency-wide generative-AI tool for staff, in June 2025 (press release 2 June 2025), ahead of a 30 June deadline (US) — [PharmTech](https://www.pharmtech.com/view/fda-deploys-agentic-ai-capabilities-for-agency-staff); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/the-fda-rolls-out-its-own-ai-to-speed-up-clinical-reviews-and-scientific-evaluations-120052323.html); [Hyman Phelps/HLC](https://hlc.com/en/publications/fda-launches-elsa-ai-tool-to-aid-drug-approvals)
- On 1 Dec 2025 FDA deployed "agentic" AI capabilities to all employees on an optional basis. Uses include pre-market reviews, validation reviews, post-market surveillance, inspections and compliance — [PDA](https://pda.org/pda-letter-portal/home/full-article/news-brief-fda-expands-ai-with-agentic-deployment); [Bloomberg Gov](https://news.bgov.com/artificial-intelligence/fda-deploys-agentic-ai-capabilities-for-agency-employees)
- Law-firm commentary: FDA's AI can review a submission "in six minutes", so firms advise sponsors to make filings machine-readable and internally consistent — [Fox Rothschild](https://www.foxrothschild.com/publications/fdas-ai-tool-can-now-review-your-submission-in-six-minutes-is-your-filing-ready). FDA plans to use AI for completeness checks, summarization, data extraction and cross-submission consistency analysis — [Hyman Phelps/HLC](https://hlc.com/en/publications/fda-advances-aipowered-review-of-medical-product-applications)
- FDA staff told reporters Elsa "confidently hallucinates" and cited non-existent studies. FDA's Chief AI Officer said staff get training and pop-up messaging to verify outputs, and acknowledged Elsa could hallucinate like other LLMs — [Medical Economics](https://www.medicaleconomics.com/view/fda-s-new-ai-tool-elsa-faces-accuracy-concerns-despite-commissioner-s-high-hopes); [Applied Clinical Trials](https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/fda-elsa-ai-tool-raises-accuracy-and-oversight-concerns); [RAPS](https://www.raps.org/resource/fda-says-elsa-can-t-hallucinate-unlikely-to-ever.html); [OECD AI incident](https://oecd.ai/en/incidents/2025-07-23-2dc4)
- Jan 2025 CDER/CBER draft guidance proposes a seven-step risk-based credibility assessment framework for AI that produces data supporting regulatory decisions (drugs/biologics, not devices). Commentary says FDA and EMA stress that sponsors must validate any AI output they rely on — [Future Medicine](https://production.futuremedicine.com/articles/advancing-the-credibility-of-ai-models-in-drug-and-biologic-development-fdas-proposed-framework); [Fulcrum Digital](https://solutions.fulcrumdigital.com/blog/where-genai-fits-inside-clinical-trial-submissions-in-2026)
- On 18 Aug 2026 the CDRH Digital Health Center of Excellence issued a discussion paper on regulating generative-AI-enabled *devices*. It covers the device itself, not AI-drafted documentation — [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/09/fda-seeks-public-feedback-on-regulatory-approach-for-generative-ai-enabled-medical-devices)
- Team-NB position paper v2 on the EU AI Act (adopted 9 Apr 2025) is about NBs assessing AI-enabled devices: designation bottleneck, a shortage risk before high-risk rules apply to devices on 2 Aug 2027, and AI-specialist recruitment. It does not address AI-written documentation (EU) — [QbD Group](https://www.qbdgroup.com/en/regulatory-updates/new-revision-of-team-nb-position-paper-on-european-ai-act); [Team-NB list](https://team-nb.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Team-NB-List-PositionPapers-20250409.pdf); Oct 2025 TEAM-NB/NBCG-Med Digital Omnibus response — [IPN](https://medicaldevices.ipn.pt/?p=3058)
- Team-NB Best Practice Guidance for technical documentation v4 (Apr 2026): documentation must show *how* the manufacturer ensures compliance with every applicable Annex I GSPR. "A simple collection of test/verification reports does not fulfil this requirement." It is non-binding (EU) — [Team-NB BPG v4](https://www.team-nb.org/team-nb-position-paper-on-bpg-for-the-submission-of-td-v4/); [DQS commentary](https://www.dqsglobal.com/en/explore/blog/improving-mdr-technical-documentation-practical-lessons-from-new-team-nb-guidance-revision)
- BSI best-practice: technical documentation must be in English, well organized and searchable — [BSI MDR best practice](https://hcms.bsigroup.com/siteassets/pdf/en/insights-and-media/insights/brochures/bsi-md-mdr-best-practice-documentation-submissions-en-gb.pdf)

### Inferences
- The regulatory risk is not "AI use is banned". It is that AI-drafted usability files may be generic, internally inconsistent or untraceable to real formative and summative evidence. FDA's own AI consistency-checking makes those defects more likely to be caught. A product built on traceability (use-related risk analysis links to hazard-related use scenarios, critical tasks, HF validation results and the ISO 14971 risk file) turns this into a selling point.
- The company should position the tool so the manufacturer's qualified HF/usability engineer remains author of record. The tool is a drafting aid under their document control.

### Gaps
- No primary FDA statement found specifically on sponsors using generative AI to write 510(k)/De Novo/PMA content, and no disclosure requirement found.
- No individual notified body (BSI, TÜV SÜD, DEKRA etc.) public policy found on AI-authored technical documentation.
- Whether the tool itself falls under the EU AI Act. It is not a medical device and not obviously an Annex III high-risk use; the company would likely be a deployer/provider of a GPAI-based system with transparency duties only. No source was found to confirm this.

## 3. Data confidentiality and security expectations; certifications for small vendors

### Takeaway
Usability files hold pre-launch design details, known use errors and risk data, which is highly sensitive IP. Buyers will expect contractual no-training and limited-retention commitments flowing down from the LLM provider, plus a security attestation. US buyers lean to SOC 2 Type II, EU buyers to ISO 27001. A solo vendor faces roughly USD 30-90k+ and 6-18 months for a first attestation, so early deals may depend on questionnaires, a single-tenant or bring-your-own-cloud/on-prem option, and a published trust/security page.

### Cited Findings
- Anthropic's commercial terms say customer content from commercial products (API, Claude for Work, Claude Gov) is not used for training. OpenAI API data is not used for training by default — [Protecto](https://www.protecto.ai/blog/openai-data-privacy/); [baa-atlas](https://baa-atlas.vercel.app/ai/anthropic-claude) (secondary; check vendor DPAs)
- Retention: OpenAI API keeps abuse-monitoring logs up to 30 days by default. Sources conflict on Anthropic's API default (7 days vs 30 days). Zero Data Retention is a negotiated or approved exception for both vendors, not standard — [developersdigest](https://www.developersdigest.tech/models/data-retention); [officechai](https://officechai.com/ai/anthropic-openai-tout-privacy-for-businesses-with-changed-data-retention-policies/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/a/5293789) (conflicting; verify in DPA)
- Most enterprise clients require SOC 2 *Type II*. ISO 27001 is the usual ask for European or global buyers — [Scrut](https://www.scrut.io/soc-2/soc-vs-iso-27001); [Mycroft](https://mycroft.io/blog/iso-27001-vs-soc-2-startups); [Advisori (DE)](https://www.advisori.de/en/blog/soc-2-vs-iso-27001-security-standard-comparison)
- Cost and time for a small company: SOC 2 Type II first year about USD 30-80k (some sources up to USD 210k), audit about USD 20-60k. ISO 27001 first year about USD 35-90k, certification audit USD 15-40k. Compliance platforms (Vanta/Drata/Secureframe) cost about USD 5-30k/yr. SOC 2 Type II takes 6-12 months, ISO 27001 9-18 months. Holding one cuts the cost of the other by about 30-40% — [GetAgency](https://blog.getagency.com/articles/soc-2-vs-iso-27001-cost-comparison); [Mycroft](https://mycroft.io/blog/iso-27001-vs-soc-2-startups) (vendor blogs; numbers vary widely)

### Inferences
- Minimum viable trust stack for a solo vendor: an enterprise LLM API with contractual no-training and ZDR where possible; an EU-region hosting option (GDPR, EU buyers); encryption plus SSO; per-customer data isolation; a DPA and subprocessor list; a security whitepaper and filled-in standard questionnaires (CAIQ/SIG-Lite); start SOC 2 Type I and then Type II via a compliance platform. Large OEMs may still block the deal until Type II exists. A self-hosted or customer-cloud deployment (customer's own Azure OpenAI/Bedrock tenant) can bypass much of the security review because data never leaves the buyer.
- Device makers whose usability files contain patient or user-study data (videos, participant data) bring GDPR and possibly HIPAA-adjacent obligations. Design the tool to avoid ingesting raw participant PII.

### Gaps
- No medtech-specific source found on which certifications device makers demand from AI-tool vendors, or on deal-closure rates without SOC 2.
- No primary vendor DPA text was fetched; the retention-period conflict is unresolved.

## 4. LLM accuracy risks for safety-critical documentation; hallucination record; human-in-the-loop patterns

### Takeaway
Hallucination is well documented even in domain-tuned, retrieval-grounded professional tools: 17-34% error rates in legal AI tools, nearly 2,000 court-filing incidents by Aug 2026, and FDA's own Elsa. For use-related risk analysis, the most dangerous failure is often omission (missing a hazardous use scenario) or plausible-but-generic content, not just fabrication. Evidence supports LLMs as brainstorming and drafting aids with mandatory expert review, not as autonomous authors.

### Cited Findings
- Stanford RegLab/HAI benchmark (2024): Lexis+ AI and Ask Practical Law AI gave incorrect information more than 17% of the time, and Westlaw AI-Assisted Research more than 34%, despite retrieval augmentation. They still did better than general GPT-4. Vendors disputed the findings — [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-trial-legal-models-hallucinate-1-out-6-queries); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/stanford-study-finds-ai-legal-research-tools-prone-to-hallucinations)
- Charlotin AI Hallucination Cases database: 120 entries in mid-2025. It reportedly reached 1,624 by 18 Jun 2026 and 1,981 by 29 Aug 2026 (1,363 US; 793 attributed to lawyers), roughly an eightfold year-on-year increase. Totals are a floor — [Business Insider NL](https://www.businessinsider.nl/ai-hallucinations-in-court-documents-are-a-growing-problem-and-data-shows-lawyers-are-responsible-for-many-of-the-errors/); [Stanford CIS](https://cyberlaw.stanford.edu/blog/2025/10/whos-submitting-ai-tainted-filings-in-court/); [The Star, Jun 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/06/15/courts-cracking-down-on-error-strewn-ai-assisted-legal-briefs); [Vaquill](https://www.vaquill.ai/blog/hallucination-tracker-charlotin-risk-manager) (the 2026 counts come from secondary reporting)
- FDA Elsa reportedly cited non-existent studies; a reviewer said "anything you don't have time to double-check is unreliable" — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/fda-is-using-an-ai-system-that-staff-say-frequently-invents-or-misrepresents-drug-research/); [Applied Clinical Trials](https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/fda-elsa-ai-tool-raises-accuracy-and-oversight-concerns)
- 2025 *Design Science* paper: an LLM-driven FMEA framework (GPT-3.5/4/4o, Gemini 1.5 Flash) reports gains in speed and accuracy in one case study, explicitly with a human-in-the-loop who validates outputs before corrective actions. Not medical-device specific — [Cambridge Design Science](https://resolve-he.cambridge.org/core/journals/design-science/article/aidriven-fmea-integration-of-large-language-models-for-faster-and-more-accurate-risk-analysis/22F110A2BF0DB4D01A69472CF17A0B43)
- 2025 *Risk Analysis* study (ChatGPT on six consumer products): strongest at divergent tasks such as brainstorming failure modes and mitigations, but showed errors, inconsistencies and generic guidance. Similar for other LLMs — [Risk Analysis via napstic](https://search.napstic.cn/literature/periodical/0220250700146519)
- A 2025 preprint shows LLMs classifying radiotherapy incident reports and drafting mitigation actions with auditable rationales — [arXiv 2511.02223](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02223v1)

### Inferences
- Human-in-the-loop patterns likely to work, drawn from the above and the CSA principle that verified outputs reduce validation burden:
  1. Grounded generation only from customer-supplied sources (use specification, user profiles, task analysis, complaint/MAUDE data), with inline citations to source.
  2. The tool proposes; the engineer disposes. Each generated hazard-related use scenario, use-error or mitigation needs explicit accept/edit/reject, and the reviewer identity is logged.
  3. Completeness checks rather than authorship: flag tasks without an analysis, risk controls without verification, and critical tasks missing from the summative protocol.
  4. Keep the tool out of risk-acceptability decisions (severity/probability ratings, benefit-risk), or flag them as human-only fields.
  5. Run a regression "golden set" per model version; show diffs when the model changes.
- Marketing should avoid "hallucination-free" or "compliant" claims, given the Stanford finding against vendors who made such claims.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found on LLMs drafting IEC 62366-1 usability files or ISO 14971 use-related risk analyses specifically; accuracy in this exact domain is unknown.
- No documented case found of an FDA 483/warning letter or NB nonconformity tied to AI-generated device documentation.

## 5. Liability and insurance for a solo vendor

### Takeaway
The worst-case exposure is downstream: a missed use hazard contributes to patient harm and a product-liability or recall claim, and the manufacturer seeks recovery from the tool vendor. The insurance market has been adding generative-AI exclusions since mid-2025, so a solo vendor must confirm that its tech E&O/cyber policy affirmatively covers AI output. Contracts must carry most of the protection: liability caps, disclaimers that outputs need qualified review, no warranty of regulatory compliance, and the customer as author of record.

### Cited Findings
- ISO (Insurance Services Office) published generative-AI exclusion forms in July 2025, with first carrier adoptions in Aug 2025. One survey counted 4,078 filed form adoptions, 3,955 of them on lines small businesses commonly buy, including E&O (US) — [CSA research note, Aug 2026](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/CSA_research_note_ai_insurance_exclusion_wave_20260830-csa-styled.pdf); [Beinsure](https://beinsure.com/genai-exclusions-spread-across-contractor-insurance-policies/); [Verisk GL exclusions](https://www.independentagent.com/?p=43061)
- Some carriers (e.g., Berkley) have introduced *absolute* AI exclusions across D&O, E&O and fiduciary policies, not limited to generative AI — [Lathrop GPM](https://www.lathropgpm.com/insights/the-ai-coverage-gap-what-new-insurance-exclusions-mean-for-your-business/); [Shumaker](https://www.shumaker.com/?p=17956)
- Startups may believe their E&O covers AI claims and discover in a crisis that an AI exclusion leaves them self-insured — [Founder Shield](https://foundershield.com/insurance-terms/definition/ai-exclusion-clause/)
- Specialist AI liability cover is emerging: Armilla (Lloyd's-backed) launched a standalone product in Apr 2025 and has raised capacity to USD 25M, and YC-backed Corgi has entered the market — [PYMNTS, 2026](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/big-insurance-backs-away-from-ai-risk-and-startups-rush-in/); [Armilla coverage news](https://lifestyle.680thefan.com/story/53400062/armilla-ai-raises-lloyds-backed-coverage-to-25m-as-traditional-insurers-retreat-from-ai-risk); [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/insurers-likely-to-exclude-gen-ai-startups-wait-in-wings)

### Inferences
- Operate through an LLC/GmbH, not personally, to shield personal assets. Buy tech E&O plus cyber with affirmative AI-output wording (or an AI endorsement or specialist policy). Expect enterprise MSAs to demand USD 1-5M E&O/cyber limits. This range is a typical industry norm; no source was found.
- The EU revised Product Liability Directive (2024/2853, which explicitly covers software) applies from Dec 2026. It could bring standalone software that contributes to damage within strict liability in the EU. This was not researched here and should be verified.

### Gaps
- No source found on actual claims against documentation-drafting AI vendors in medtech.
- No premium figures found for solo AI vendors in regulated sectors.

## 6. Employment risks for an employed R&D project manager starting a same-industry side project

### Takeaway
The biggest risks are contractual and fiduciary, not non-competes. Invention-assignment clauses typically capture anything related to the employer's business or R&D, even if built on your own time. The duty of loyalty bars competing (not preparing) while employed. Confidential information or templates from the day job must never be used. The FTC's nationwide non-compete ban is dead, but state law (US) and German HGB rules (EU) still govern post-employment restraints. Get written employer consent or a carve-out, and have an employment lawyer review the specific contract.

### Cited Findings
- FTC non-compete rule: finalized 2024, never took effect, set aside by courts. On 5 Sept 2025 the FTC voted 3-1 to dismiss its appeals (Ryan LLC v. FTC, 5th Cir.; Properties of the Villages v. FTC, 11th Cir.) and accepted the vacatur. It continues case-by-case enforcement against abusive non-competes (US) — [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/ftc-shifts-approach-to-non-compete-enforcement); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/ftc-wont-enforce-its-noncompete-rule-noncompete-enforcement-continues); [Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2025/09/09/ftc-vacates-non-compete-clause-rule-and-dismisses-court-appeals/)
- California Labor Code §2870: assignment clauses cannot reach inventions developed entirely on the employee's own time without employer equipment, supplies, facilities or trade secrets. The exception: inventions that relate to the employer's business or actual or demonstrably anticipated R&D, or that result from work done for the employer. The employee bears the burden of proof. §2872 requires written notice. Similar statutes exist in other states (not verified here) (US-CA) — [California Labor Code §2870 (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/2020/code-lab/division-3/chapter-2/article-3-5/section-2870/); [terms.law](https://terms.law/CA-Remote-Hub/ca-remote-ip-confidentiality.html)
- Whitewater West v. Alleshouse (Fed. Cir. 2020): a post-employment invention assignment clause was held void under Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §16600 — [Pearl Cohen](https://www.pearlcohen.com/federal-circuit-holds-contractual-assignment-provision-void-under-california-law/); [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2020/11/23/federal-circuit-reverses-district-court-sides-inventors-analysis-california-employment-contract-law/id=127492/)
- Duty of loyalty: every employee owes it, contract or not. In most states it can forbid running a business that competes with the employer. An employee may *prepare* a competing business while employed but may not *begin competing* until departure. Fiduciary-level staff may have to disclose opportunities first (US) — [UBG Law](https://businessinsights.ubglaw.com/2021/02/employees-and-officers-what-duties-do-they-owe-to-their-employer-in-the-absence-of-a-contract/); [FindLaw](https://www.findlaw.com/?p=47076)
- Washington Supreme Court (David v. Freedom Vans, 2025) construed the duty-of-loyalty exception to the state's anti-moonlighting law narrowly. State law varies (US-WA) — [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/washington-supreme-court-limits-duty-loyalty-exception-anti-moonlighting-law)
- Germany: a post-contractual non-compete binds only if the employer pays at least 50% of the last contractual remuneration (§74 HGB); a clause with no compensation is void. During employment, employees are under statutory non-competition duties (EU-DE) — [Bird & Bird 2025](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/germany/nachvertragliche-wettbewerbsverbote-deutsche-regelungen-im-internationalen-kontext); [Mayer Brown](https://mayerbrown.com/fr/insights/publications/2024/07/restrictive-covenants-germany); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/germany/Employment-and-HR/633728/A-Post-Contractual-Non-Compete-Clause-That-Does-Not-Provide-For-The-Statutory-Non-Compete-Compensation-Is-Void-Even-If-It-Contains-A-Severability-Clause)

### Inferences
- An R&D project manager at a device maker who builds a usability-documentation tool is squarely within the "relates to employer's business or anticipated R&D" exception if the employer has HF/usability work. Software that is arguably a service to the industry rather than a device may help, but this carries real risk. Mitigations: a written side-business approval with an IP carve-out; no employer devices, time, networks, templates, SOPs, usability files or know-how; a clean-room build documented with dated commits on personal equipment; no selling to the current employer or its direct competitors while employed; avoid soliciting colleagues.
- Conflict of interest: the employer may be a natural customer or may evaluate competing tools. The person should disclose and recuse from vendor-selection decisions.
- For the EU (Germany), the Employee Inventions Act (ArbnErfG) and Nebentätigkeit approval clauses are also relevant. These were not researched here.

### Gaps
- No source reviewed for German ArbnErfG or §60 HGB as applied to software side projects; no other US state invention statutes (WA, IL, MN, etc.) verified.
- The person's actual jurisdiction and contract terms are unknown; that is decisive.
