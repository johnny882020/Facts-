# Market Size and Demand for Medical Device Usability Engineering / Human Factors (HF) Work

Research date: 2026-11-05. Scope: input to evaluating an AI copilot SaaS that drafts IEC 62366-1 usability files. Note: emergobyul.com and thefdalawblog.com could not be fetched from this session (network allow-list), so claims from those sites rely on search-result snippets and are marked accordingly. Most figures below come from secondary sources (vendors, consultancies, market-research publishers); estimates are flagged.

## 1. How many manufacturers exist, and how many submissions per year need HF/usability documentation?

### Takeaway
Roughly 3,200 510(k) clearances plus a few dozen De Novo grants and original PMAs per year in the US (CY2025), and more than 38,000 medtech companies in Europe (about 90% SMEs). The FDA establishment registry is far larger (on the order of hundreds of thousands of entries, unverified) but includes non-manufacturers. HF documentation of some kind is expected in essentially every 510(k)/De Novo/PMA/HDE under FDA's 2026 final guidance, and usability engineering is mandatory for all EU MDR devices. Only a subset needs a full HF validation (summative) report.

### Cited Findings
- CY2025 US marketing authorizations from openFDA data: 3,198 510(k) clearances, 27 De Novo grants, 38 original PMA approvals. 510(k)s were 98.0% of new authorizations. Over 2016–2025: 30,669 clearances, 322 De Novo grants, 281 original PMAs. 2023 and 2024 had 47 De Novo grants each. (Vendor analysis.) [Pure Global](https://www.pureglobal.com/blog-posts/fda-cleared-vs-approved-vs-granted-510k-pma-de-novo)
- A second source counts 3,195 510(k) clearances for 2025, built from FDA 510(k) public files. [510kdatabase.net](https://510kdatabase.net/clearances-2025)
- FDA establishment registry: one unverified blog puts it at about 324,000 establishments worldwide, from openFDA. It includes all establishment types (contract manufacturers, repackagers, relabelers, specification developers, foreign sites), not only legal manufacturers who submit. Treat as an upper bound, not a count of HF buyers. [ai-analytics.org](https://ai-analytics.org/writing/fda-device-establishments/)
- Annual FDA registration is mandatory for owners and operators of establishments producing or distributing devices for the US. The FY2025 registration fee was $9,280. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-device-registration-and-listing)
- Europe has more than 38,000 medtech companies. Germany has the most, followed by Italy, the UK, France and Switzerland. SMEs are about 90% of firms, most with fewer than 50 employees. The sector employs more than 930,000 people. [MedTech Europe Data Hub](https://www.medtecheurope.org/datahub/introduction/); [MedTech Europe Facts & Figures 2025 press release](https://www.medtecheurope.org/2025/09/04/medtech-europe-unveils-facts-figures-2025-a-snapshot-of-innovation-and-impact-in-europe/)
- FDA's final HF content guidance (May 2026) applies to 510(k), De Novo, PMA and HDE submissions and sets a risk-based framework of HF submission categories. [NSF](https://www.nsf.org/life-science-regulatory-news/fda-issues-final-guidance-on-human-factors-information-in-medical-device-submissions); [Exponent](https://www.exponent.com/article/fda-finalizes-human-factors-guidance-medical-device-marketing-submissions)
- Under EU MDR, usability engineering per IEC 62366-1:2015+AMD1:2020 is the harmonised route to GSPR compliance, and notified bodies expect it to be integrated into risk management and technical documentation. This applies to non-active devices too. [TÜV NORD](https://www.tuv-nord.com/pl/en/uslugi/medical-devices/mdr-knowledge-hub/usability-engineering-of-non-active-medical-devices-in-light-of-the-mdr/); [VDE](https://www.vde.com/topics-en/health/consulting/usability-engineering-for-medical-devices-iec-62366)

### Inferences
- The US flow is about 3,250 new authorizations per year (CY2025). 510(k) counts are noisy because many clearances are modifications, and many filers submit several. Distinct US submitting firms per year are likely in the low thousands (estimate, not sourced).
- In the EU, the base is the roughly 38,000 companies, all of which need a usability engineering file for every device under MDR (and IVDR has a parallel usability requirement). This includes legacy devices being transitioned to MDR, and it is a stock of files to update, not only new ones. That makes the EU arguably the larger "document volume" market for a copilot, even though the US is the bigger HF services spend.
- Under FDA's tiered categories, every submission needs at least an HF summary or justification, while only higher-risk categories need a full HF validation report. The share of 510(k)s in each category is not published (see Gaps).
- Rough serviceable market, labelled estimate: if 30–50% of about 3,200 US submissions per year carry meaningful HF documentation, that is about 1,000–1,600 US HF files per year, plus EU MDR files across the 38,000-company base.

### Gaps
- No authoritative FDA count of distinct device manufacturers (as opposed to establishments) for 2025/2026. The openFDA registration endpoint could be queried directly for owner/operator counts by establishment type ("Manufacturer", "Specification Developer").
- No public data on how many 510(k)s fall into each FDA HF submission category, or how many include a full HF validation report.
- No count of EU MDR certificates or technical documentation assessments per year was found in this pass. NANDO and the EU MDR implementation dashboards would be the place to look.
- No global manufacturer count was found.
- The 27 De Novo figure for 2025 is lower than the 47 per year in 2023–2024. It may be real, or it may be a data-lag artifact. Verify against FDA's De Novo database.

## 2. What does HF/usability work cost today, and how big is the HF consulting market?

### Takeaway
Public pricing is scarce. Anecdotal practitioner figures put formative testing at $60K–$120K and a summative validation at low six figures (US). Participant honoraria run from $150 to $1,500+ per session. Internal HF engineers earn about $85K–$105K base in the US. Market-research firms size HF/usability engineering services at about USD 1.1B (2023–2026), growing about 7% a year (low-confidence projections). Emergo by UL is the dominant consolidator, built through acquisitions of Wiklund R&D (2012), Emergo (2017) and Medical Device Usability (2018).

### Cited Findings
- Formalized formative usability testing: $60K–$120K and 1–3 months, depending on rounds and user groups. Summative validation: "low six figures" and 4–6 months. (Anecdotal; single practitioner LinkedIn post.) [Lana Hasher, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/lrhasher)
- Participant honoraria, same source: nurses $150–$400 per session; specialist physicians (surgeons, interventional cardiologists) $500–$1,500+. [Lana Hasher, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/lrhasher)
- Sample-size drivers: FDA's minimum of 15 participants per distinct user group for summative testing; some practitioners suggest 20–25 per user type. Formative tests typically use 5–8 (per IEC 62366-2) or 8–12 participants. [RAPS Connect discussion](https://connect.raps.org:443/discussion/usability-summative-testing-re-testing?hlmlt=VT); [MDDI](https://www.mddionline.com/design-engineering/usability-testing-validating-user-interface-design)
- Usability engineering file and procedure templates sell for about $250 (file template) and $150 (process procedure). This shows the low end of the documentation-only market. [I3C Global](https://www.i3cglobal.com/usability-engineering-file-template/)
- Johner Institute sells end-to-end HF services (usability file, recruiting, testing, IEC 62366-1/FDA documentation) with labs in Frankfurt and the US. No public prices. [Johner Institute](https://www.johner-institute.com/consulting-approval/services/human-factors-engineering/)
- US contract HF/usability engineer rates (W-2, worker pay, not consultancy bill rates): $55–$70/hr (Concord USA, senior); $74–$84/hr (12-month biopharma/medical device role); $46–$60/hr (Insight Global, California). [Concord USA](https://concordusa.applytojob.com/apply/jobs/details/1oskzruP9x); [3KC/Catsone](https://3kc.catsone.com/careers/2010/jobs/16823242-Human-Factors-EngineerUsability-Engineering-Biopharma-Medical-Device-Remote-JP15469); [Insight Global](https://jobs.insightglobal.com/find_a_job/california/job-418412/)
- Internal FTE salary: Payscale lists an average base of $85,533 (10th–90th percentile $71K–$126K) for HF engineers with medical-device skills. The San Jose average is $103,481 and Boston $87,550. Zippia's all-industry HF engineer average is $96,427. [Payscale](https://payscale.com/research/US/Job=Human_Factors_Engineer/Salary/ffe667ff/Medical-Devices); [Payscale San Jose](https://www.payscale.com/research/US/Job=Human_Factors_Engineer/Salary/031615d3/San-Jose-CA-Medical-Devices); [Zippia](https://www.zippia.com/human-factors-engineer-jobs/salary)
- 77% of HF engineers hold at least a master's degree (ERI via USF Health). [USF Health](https://www.usfhealthonline.com/?p=30429)
- Market size (market-research projections, low confidence): Spherical Insights puts the global HF and usability engineering services market at USD 1.09B in 2023, reaching about USD 2.10B by 2033 at a 6.78% CAGR. Roots Analysis gives USD 1.09B in 2026, rising to USD 1.97B by 2035 (about 7% CAGR). An earlier Roots report projected the medical-device HFE/usability testing segment at USD 1B by 2030. [Spherical Insights](https://sphericalinsights.com/reports/human-factors-and-usability-engineering-services-market); [Roots Analysis](https://www.rootsanalysis.com/press-releases/human-factors-and-usability-engineering-services-market-growth.html); [Roots Analysis 2020](https://www.rootsanalysis.com/press-releases/222-the-human-factors-engineering-and-usability-testing-services-market-for-medical-devices-is-projected-to-reach-usd-1-billion-by-2030-claims-roots-analysis.html)
- Consolidation: UL acquired Emergo (closed April 21, 2017) and the UK firm Medical Device Usability (closed August 31, 2018), folding the latter into Emergo by UL. Wiklund Research & Design was acquired in 2012 and forms the core of Emergo by UL's Human Factors Research & Design team. Deal terms were not disclosed. [UL](https://www.ul.com/news/ul-acquires-medical-device-usability-medical-human-factors-business); [UL – Emergo](https://www.ul.com/news/ul-acquires-emergo-expands-regulatory-services-footprint-medical-device-industry); [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/ul-absorbs-u-k-based-medtech-human-factors-firm)

### Inferences
- Labelled estimate: a full US HF program for a moderate-risk device (2–3 formative rounds plus a summative study plus the HFE/UE file) plausibly costs $200K–$400K in external spend. That is $60–120K per formative round times 2, plus a $100–200K summative study. Documentation (use specification, task analysis, URRA, protocols, reports) is likely 20–40% of consultant hours. That share is the part a drafting copilot targets, while recruiting, moderation and labs remain services.
- Labelled estimate: a fully loaded internal HF FTE costs about $120K–$150K a year (the $85–105K base times 1.3–1.4). Contract rates of $55–84/hr W-2 imply consultancy bill rates likely in the $150–$250/hr range (not sourced).
- The roughly USD 1.1B services market spans all HF services, testing included. A documentation-copilot SaaS would capture a fraction of it. Template sellers ($150–$250) anchor the low end, and eQMS tools (e.g., Greenlight Guru) are adjacent incumbents.

### Gaps
- No public price lists from Emergo by UL, Core Human Factors, Design Science, Bold Insight or Kymanox were found. Revenues for these firms are not public.
- No dedicated medtech HF salary survey with HF broken out. The MD+DI 2025 Medtech Salary Survey (385 respondents) is behind registration. [MDDI](https://www.mddionline.com/business/medtech-salary-survey-2025-full-report)
- No sourced consultancy hourly bill rate for HF.
- No EU-specific cost benchmarks were found.

## 3. Regulatory drivers, including 2025–2026 changes

### Takeaway
The biggest 2026 change: FDA finalized "Content of Human Factors Information in Medical Device Marketing Submissions" on May 29, 2026 (effective August 1, 2026), replacing the December 2022 draft. It adds a new Decision Point D on whether HF validation data must be submitted, and introduces a new HF report structure tied to eSTAR. The QMSR took effect February 2, 2026, incorporating ISO 13485:2016, with a new inspection program. IEC 62366-1 remains the 2015+AMD1:2020 edition. IEC TS 62366-2 is being revised, and a new TS 62366-3 on AI/ML is in development.

### Cited Findings
- The final FDA HF content guidance was issued May 29, 2026, with implementation August 1, 2026 (60-day transition). It replaces the December 2022 draft and covers 510(k), De Novo, PMA and HDE. The FDA town hall transcript confirms the dates. [FDA town hall transcript](https://www.fda.gov/media/193938/download); [NSF](https://www.nsf.org/life-science-regulatory-news/fda-issues-final-guidance-on-human-factors-information-in-medical-device-submissions); [Exponent](https://www.exponent.com/article/fda-finalizes-human-factors-guidance-medical-device-marketing-submissions)
- The final guidance adds Decision Point D to the flowchart, which evaluates whether HF validation test data should be submitted. Commentators disagree on its effect. One law firm says it may increase the number of devices needing an HF validation study. Another view says it may reduce the testing burden in some submissions, but FDA may scrutinize HF more closely during QMSR inspections. [MedDeviceGuide](https://meddeviceguide.com/blog/fda-human-factors-final-guidance-2026-submission-categories-decision-point-d); [The FDA Law Blog (snippet only; fetch blocked)](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2026/06/fda-issues-final-guidance-for-content-of-human-factors-information-in-medical-device-submissions/)
- The final guidance is a companion to the 2016 guidance "Applying Human Factors and Usability Engineering to Medical Devices", not a replacement. FDA updated eSTAR so applicants select an HF Submission Category with supporting information. Manufacturers using the old 2016 report outline after August 2026 are expected to get AI requests to restructure. (Snippet from Emergo by UL; fetch blocked.) [Emergo by UL](https://emergobyul.com/news/key-updates-final-fda-guidance-content-human-factors-information-medical-device-marketing)
- On the December 2022 draft: a regulatory law blog called it "a bridge too far" and said FDA HF reviews already generate a "good percentage" of burdensome AI requests in 510(k) reviews. [The FDA Law Blog, Feb 2023](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2023/02/the-new-fda-draft-human-factors-guidance-a-bridge-too-far/)
- QMSR: FDA stopped using QSIT and moved to Compliance Program 7382.850 on February 2, 2026. The QMSR incorporates ISO 13485:2016 by reference. FDA investigators may review records created before February 2, 2026. [FDA Quality and Compliance](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-advice-comprehensive-regulatory-assistance/quality-and-compliance-medical-devices); [FDA QMSR FAQ](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/quality-system-qs-regulationmedical-device-good-manufacturing-practices/quality-management-system-regulation-final-rule-amending-quality-system-regulation-frequently-asked); [Pure Global](https://www.pureglobal.com/blog-posts/fda-inspection-readiness-checklist-for-medical-device-manufacturers)
- Under QMSR, inspectors assess whether design outputs, verification, validation and changes align with identified risks and intended use, using ISO 13485's design and development framework. [NAMSA](https://namsa.com/?p=35474)
- IEC 62366-1: the current version is the 2015 first edition plus Amendment 1 (2020). The IEC webstore lists a stability date of 2026. No new edition was found. [IEC webstore](https://webstore.iec.ch/publication/67220)
- IEC TS 62366-2 Ed.1 (guidance) is in development, at CD ballot in 2025 (voting closed November 7, 2025, per one national tracker). IEC TS 62366-3 Ed.1, a new project on usability for AI/ML, is at CD stage with a September 25, 2026 date. These come from national standards-body trackers, so verify on the IEC site. [iss.rs tracker](https://iss.rs/en/project/show/iec:proj:131875); [dps.gov.al tracker](https://dps.gov.al/en/project/show/iec:proj:121835)
- EU MDR: usability is part of the GSPRs (Annex I, including reducing use-error risk and considering the technical knowledge, experience, education and training of intended users). It is harmonised via EN 62366-1 (DIN EN 62366-1:2021-08). [VDE](https://www.vde.com/topics-en/health/consulting/usability-engineering-for-medical-devices-iec-62366)

### Inferences
- August 1, 2026 marks a near-term demand event. Every US submission from then on must map to the new HF category and the new report structure in eSTAR, so manufacturers and consultants need to rework templates. A copilot that natively outputs the new structure has a timely wedge.
- The QMSR moves usability into ISO 13485 design-and-development inspection expectations. That plausibly raises demand for well-traceable, inspection-ready usability files (use specification, URRA linked to the ISO 14971 risk file), not just submission summaries.
- IEC 62366-1 hitting its 2026 stability date, plus TS 62366-2/-3 revisions (especially AI/ML), signal future standard churn, which is a recurring reason to update templates.

### Gaps
- Could not read the full final FDA guidance text in this pass. The exact category definitions and report outline should be verified from the FDA guidance page.
- No primary source found on how CP 7382.850 treats HF/usability in inspections.
- No 2025–2026 EU-level MDCG guidance specific to usability was identified.

## 4. Evidence of the problem: deficiencies, recalls and MAUDE use-error data

### Takeaway
Evidence of pain is real but mostly qualitative or old. FDA asks for additional information on more than three-quarters of 510(k)s in the first review cycle, and HF is widely described as a significant source of those requests, but no public quantified HF share exists. Commonly cited themes: incomplete URRA, missing critical-task identification, submitting only a summative report, and test realism. In the EU, notified bodies most often flag usability handled separately from risk management. Use error accounts for about 7–31% of coded MAUDE reports, depending on dataset. A widely repeated claim that "nearly half of recalls are due to poor UI design" could not be verified.

### Cited Findings
- More than three-quarters of 510(k)s needed to provide additional information in the first review cycle, falling to 6% in the second cycle (MDUFA performance data). [RAPS](https://www.raps.org/resource/medical-device-performance-goals-fda-offers-a-qua.html)
- FDA HF reviews generate "a good percentage" of burdensome AI requests in 510(k) reviews, often because manufacturers are unfamiliar with HF reviewers' vocabulary and expectations (qualitative). [The FDA Law Blog](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2023/02/the-new-fda-draft-human-factors-guidance-a-bridge-too-far/)
- FDA does not publish a ranked tally of HF deficiencies. Recurring failure modes inferred from guidance and outcomes include incomplete URRAs, missing critical-task identification, insufficient validation data, and lack of realism or representativeness in validation testing. [Rhizome AI analysis](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/human-factors-deficiencies-in-fda-premarket); [Thrive Thinking](https://thrivethinking.com/2026/03/12/510k-deficiency-letter/)
- Unverified claim: "approximately ninety percent" of HF testing reports in 510(k)s are deficient, often because only the summative report is submitted. It comes from a commercial training blog and is not traceable to FDA. Treat with caution. [Medical Device Academy](https://medicaldeviceacademy.com/human-factors-questions/)
- EU MDR: treating the usability engineering process as independent of risk management is "one of the most frequently seen problems" in technical documentation assessments. [TÜV NORD](https://www.tuv-nord.com/pl/en/uslugi/medical-devices/mdr-knowledge-hub/usability-engineering-of-non-active-medical-devices-in-light-of-the-mdr/)
- Notified bodies request more evidence when usability-to-risk links are thin, and expect summative acceptance criteria defined before testing (practitioner). [DEV Community](https://dev.to/priya_nair_ree/where-usability-files-actually-fail-and-how-to-stop-firefighting-later-2pfg)
- Clinical evidence, not usability, is the single most frequent MDR technical-file deficiency category. [MedDeviceGuide](https://meddeviceguide.com/blog/eu-mdr-notified-body-technical-file-deficiencies-common-nonconformities-guide)
- MAUDE: use error made up 28.1% of reports labelled with device problem codes (2010–2018 data, HFES 2020). [HFES Proceedings / SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/ref/10.1177/2327857920091024)
- MAUDE reporter variation (2005–2022; published online November 2024): user facilities reported device-issue codes 54.3% and use codes 30.8%. Across all reports, device issues were 36.3%, unknown 32% and clinical 19.3%. [Mishali et al., Univ. of Haifa](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/understanding-variation-among-medical-device-reporting-sources-a-/)
- AI-enabled devices (JAMIA, 266 MAUDE events 2015–2021): 7% were "use problems", but use problems were four times more likely than device problems to cause patient harm. [HealthExec](https://healthexec.com/node/237841)
- Recalls: design was the leading recall cause at 36% (FDA FY2003–FY2012 recall trend analysis, secondary summary; old data). [JD Supra](https://production.jdsupra.com/legalnews/unpacking-averages-common-root-causes-1688072)
- Unverified claim: "nearly half of all device recalls" were due to poor UI design (trade article; underlying FDA data not found). [MassDevice](https://www.massdevice.com/focusing-on-usability-can-limit-medical-device-recalls-heres-how/)
- Infusion pumps were reportedly linked to a large share of serious adverse events in 2005–2009, many tied to programming or usability problems (secondary; historical). [NAMSA](https://namsa.com/human-factors-usability-engineering-in-medical-devices/)

### Inferences
- The demand signal for a copilot rests on three things: (a) high AI-request rates overall, with HF repeatedly named as a major contributor; (b) recurring documentation-quality failures (URRA completeness, critical-task traceability, linkage to risk management), which a drafting tool can address directly; and (c) a 2026 format change that forces rework.
- Use error is a meaningful share of device adverse events (about 20–30% of coded MAUDE reports in general datasets), which keeps HF high on regulators' agendas. Under-reporting and coding ambiguity (32% "unknown") make the true share uncertain.

### Gaps
- No public quantified share of FDA AI letters, RTA holds or notified-body findings that cite HF. FDA's MDUFA reports do not break out deficiency topics. A FOIA request or analysis of published 510(k) review memos (De Novo decision summaries) could produce this.
- No 2023–2026 FDA recall analysis attributing recalls to use error or UI design was found. The FDA recall database could be coded by root cause ("device design", "labeling", "use error") for a fresh estimate.
- No 2024–2026 FDA-published MAUDE use-error statistics were found.
- Emergo by UL survey or report data could not be accessed (site blocked from this session); emergobyul.com would need to be allowed for follow-up.
