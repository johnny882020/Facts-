## 1. Which AI use cases work and which stall, and why?

### Takeaway
Narrow, well-defined, data-rich use cases (demand forecasting, invoice/billing exception triage) show the most evidence of value; end-to-end, agentic and generative use cases mostly sit in pilots. Surveys from Gartner and BCG converge on a deployment/integration/operating-model problem rather than a model-quality problem.

### Cited Findings
- Gartner survey (140 senior supply chain leaders, Nov 2025; press release dated 2026-05-06): most organizations are still testing isolated pilots rather than overhauling end-to-end processes; only 17% pursue an immediate AI-driven transformational redesign of workflows, 83% apply AI incrementally or scale gradually — [Gartner press release](https://gcomdr.pdo.aws.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-06-gartner-survey-shows-ai-is-not-driving-supply-chain-operating-model-transformation); also [SC247 summary](https://www.supplychain247.com/article/gartner-ai-supply-chain-operating-models-survey)
- Gartner (survey of 135 senior leaders, Jan-Apr 2026, per CCJ): 55% of supply chain chiefs are unclear about their AI returns, while AI is 67% of supply chain digital investments — [SC247](https://www.supplychain247.com/article/gartner-supply-chain-chiefs-unclear-ai-investment-returns/radial). Note: search summary flagged that 55% also appears in an unrelated Gartner survey (entry-level hiring), and sources disagree on survey dates/sample (135 vs 140 leaders).
- Only 17% of AI pilots launched by supply chain organizations scale successfully, per Gartner's Snigdha Dewal in a webinar as reported by CCJ — [CCJ](https://www.ccjdigital.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/article/15832469/ai-implementation-strategy-why-fragmentation-blocks-scalability). Single secondary source; verify. Separate Gartner article says 17% of organizations (all functions) have deployed AI at scale and frames human adoption as the limiter — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/supply-chain-ai-adoption)
- BCG, AI in Logistics survey (Jan 2026, 180+ LSPs and shippers; press release 27 Mar 2026): ~13% of LSPs report measurable financial impact from AI, lower for shippers; ~40% of LSPs are past pilots but only ~1 in 10 have AI embedded in core operations at scale; BCG calls it a deployment problem, with unclear ROI and capability gaps outranking cost/technical complexity — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/why-ai-isnt-delivering-roi-logistics); [BCG press](https://www.bcg.com/press/27march2026-ai-expectations-rise-in-logistics-scaled-adoption-remains-limited)
- BCG Southeast Asia brief: only 7% of shippers can point to measurable supply chain improvements; about 1% have AI embedded in core logistics processes — [BCG PDF](https://web-assets.bcg.com/d2/23/bd2b587b4d1da0aa612b9d670d57/how-southeast-asia-logistics-gain-from-aggregating-ai-opportunities-vf.pdf). Regional (SE Asia) brief; may not generalize.
- BCG Supply Chain Planning 2026 (181 leaders, per a search summary): ~20% report meaningful AI value so far; as little as 7% from agentic/gen AI — I could not locate the primary page; treat as lower confidence (reported via search summary only).
- BCG executive survey: 97% of logistics executives rank AI a strategic priority (sample of 30 leading logistics players per one summary) — [Supply Chain Connect/BCG via search](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/why-ai-isnt-delivering-roi-logistics). Small sample; flagged.
- BCG broader: 26% of companies get significant value from AI, 74% fail to scale — [SC247](https://www.supplychain247.com/article/companies-struggle-create-value-artificial-intelligence-bcg-survery/blogs) (cross-industry).
- McKinsey State of AI 2025 (1,993 respondents, 105 countries, June-July 2025): ~88% use AI in at least one function; only about one-third have begun scaling enterprise-wide; ~a quarter are scaling an agentic system in at least one function, usually one or two — [secondary summary](https://siliconcanals.com/m-mckinseys-2025-global-ai-survey-88-of-organizations-now-use-ai-in-at-least-one-function-up-from-78-but-most-are-still-stuck-in-pilot-mode-and-only-a-minority-can-point-to-any-real-impact/). Cross-industry, not supply-chain-specific; primary not read.
- MIT NANDA "GenAI Divide" (Aug 2025; 150 interviews, 350 employee survey, 300 deployments): ~95% of gen AI pilots show no measurable P&L impact; vendor-bought/partnered tools succeed ~67% vs internal builds about one-third as often; biggest ROI in back-office automation — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/). Cross-industry, modest sample, framing varies by outlet; not supply-chain-specific.
- Deloitte (2025 tech value survey, ~550 leaders, cross-industry): 84% of those investing in AI/gen AI say they gain ROI — contrast with BCG/MIT low figures; difference is likely sample and definition of "value" — [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/digital-transformation/ai-tech-investment-roi.html)
- Working: demand forecasting described as most mature use case, adoption 87% among leading organizations (secondary claim); Gartner-attributed: 15% forecast accuracy improvement yields 2-3% bottom-line gain — [TransImpact](https://transimpact.com/blog/how-ai-delivers-value-in-the-modern-supply-chain). Vendor-adjacent 20-40% forecast error reduction claim is unverified.
- Gartner: 72% of supply chain orgs have deployed gen AI but most see middling productivity/ROI; Gartner analyst predicts 60% of supply chain digital adoption efforts will fail to deliver promised value by 2028, largely from insufficient change management (secondary) — [Gartner 2026 predictions](https://www.gartner.com/en/supply-chain/insights/beyond-supply-chain-blog/beyond-supply-chain-2026-predictions); [TransImpact](https://transimpact.com/blog/how-ai-delivers-value-in-the-modern-supply-chain)
- Blockers named: data fragmentation/quality, legacy-system integration, trust gaps, talent shortage — [Supply Chain Connect](https://www.supplychainconnect.com/supply-chain-technology/article/55326402/agentic-ai-in-supply-chain-and-logistics); siloed agents optimize locally at the expense of the network — [SupplyChainBrain](https://www.supplychainbrain.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/blog/15882228/how-supply-chains-can-responsibly-implement-agentic-ai-at-scale); unclear decision ownership rather than model intelligence — [SCMR](https://www.scmr.com/article/after-ai-agent-pilots-underperformed-resetting-supply-chain-automation-for-operational-impact)
- Invoice exceptions (working example): Worldwide Express reports ~14% of LTL invoices fail three-way match; AI agents reportedly cut active freight billing exceptions by 75% and released a one-time $10M of cash flow (self-reported; secondary write-up) — [AWS blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/how-worldwide-express-automated-freight-exceptions-with-amazon-bedrock/); [Remio](https://www.remio.ai/post/worldwide-express-freight-automation-cuts-billing-exceptions-with-amazon-bedrock)
- Tariff/funding effect (RELEX, Jan 2026, 514 leaders, vendor survey): 86% say tariffs already affected operations; 60% say tariff uncertainty has constrained AI/automation funding — [RELEX](https://www.relexsolutions.com/news/relex-report-86-of-supply-chain-leaders-impacted-by-tariffs-and-economic-pressures-as-companies-split-on-pricing-and-inventory-strategy/)

### Inferences
- The pattern of high adoption but low scaled value (Gartner 17%, BCG ~13%/10%, McKinsey ~1/3) suggests the bottleneck is process redesign, data integration and decision ownership. Deloitte's 84% vs BCG/MIT figures should be presented as a definitional divergence, not consensus.
- Use cases with a bounded decision, structured data and a measurable cash outcome (invoice matching, forecasting) scale best; cross-functional, cross-system ones stall.

### Gaps
- No Fortune 500-specific breakdown found in any survey; no verified Accenture supply chain AI ROI figure (search returned nothing).
- No 2026 Gartner Hype Cycle phase placements retrieved (documents dated 23 Jun and 24 Jul 2026 exist but only abstracts surfaced).
- Primary documents not read due to fetch restrictions.

## 2. What supply chain leaders say they still cannot do

### Takeaway
End-to-end visibility is the biggest self-reported gap, especially beyond Tier 1; survey numbers vary widely by definition (16% to 97%).

### Cited Findings
- Only 16% of 301 leaders (N. America and Europe, 2026) report end-to-end real-time visibility from order through return (Cleo survey, secondary write-up) — [SC247](https://www.supplychain247.com/article/most-companies-say-their-supply-chains-work-the-data-says-otherwise/software). Contradicted by FedEx's report that 97% of decision-makers say they have end-to-end shipment visibility — [FedEx](https://digital-blog.fedex.com/3-key-insights-from-the-future-of-logistics-intelligence-report). Likely a definitional gap (shipment vs order-to-return).
- KPMG: 38% of supply chain leaders say processes are strongly/fully integrated with enterprise-wide planning — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/future-supply-chain.html)
- Multi-tier: Achilles 2026: only 6% have full Tier 2/3 visibility, nearly half limited or none beyond direct suppliers — [Hellenic Shipping News](https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/achilles-global-survey-reveals-major-visibility-gaps-across-supply-chains-as-regulatory-pressure-drives-risk-management-priorities/); McKinsey Jan 2026 (via The Chain, secondary): 95% see Tier 1, 42% see Tier 2+, down since 2022 — [SC247](https://www.supplychain247.com/article/supply-chain-visibility-gaps-tier-2-suppliers/risk_management); Integrity Next 2026: 11% of European and 17% of US companies have multi-tier visibility — [Integrity Next](https://www.integritynext.com/resources/blog/article/supply-chain-risk-management-in-europe-insights-from-the-2026-risk-resilience-study); Dun & Bradstreet: 11% of US manufacturers assess full multitier chain, 47% blocked by lack of data — [D&B](https://www.dnb.com/en-us/blog/supplier-risk/building-resilience-supply-chain-risk-insights-for-2026.html); EcoVadis 2026: 12% see >50% of Tier 2 — [Jaggaer](https://www.jaggaer.com/blog/why-supply-chain-visibility-breaks-down-beyond-tier-1-in-2026)
- Planning under tariffs: see RELEX figures above; Netstock found nearly three-quarters of US SMBs lengthened inventory planning horizons — [Netstock](https://www.netstock.com/research/2026-tariff-impact-report/). No evidence found on AI forecast accuracy specifically degrading under tariffs.
- Freight claims/audit: vendors (Kognitos, Loop, Upwell, Shipwell) are only now automating claims evidence packages and carrier dispute handling; Shipwell claims up to 5% freight spend savings (vendor claim) — [Kognitos](https://www.kognitos.com/use-case/freight-claim-evidence-package-automation/); [Loop](https://www.loop.com/article/a-deep-dive-with-loops-exception-agent-automatically-manage-freight-disputes); [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/freight-billing-gets-ai-boost-as-kleinschmidt-backs-upwell). Remio notes software can extract an invoice correctly yet misjudge whether the charge belongs.

### Inferences
- Visibility is weakest where data lives outside the enterprise (sub-tier suppliers, carriers), which AI cannot fix without data sharing.
- Claims is early-stage; evidence is vendor-sourced only.

### Gaps
- No quantified enterprise survey on claims or exception-handling backlog found.
- No independent data on AI planning performance in tariff shocks.

## 3. Agentic AI in supply chain: deployments, failure modes, audit/hallucination concerns

### Takeaway
Agentic AI is heavily marketed, with few independently verified production outcomes; analysts predict high cancellation rates and stress governance, audit trails and constrained autonomy.

### Cited Findings
- Gartner (June 2025): over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027 (cost, unclear value, weak risk controls); only ~130 of thousands of vendors offer real agentic features ("agent washing") — [SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/agentic-ai/gartner-more-than-40-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-in-next-few-years/). Cross-industry.
- Gartner (Feb 2026, 509 supply chain leaders): 55% expect agentic AI to reduce entry-level hiring; agentic AI named top factor reshaping strategy — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-02-25-gartner-survey-shows-55-percent-of-supply-chain-leaders-expect-agentic-ai-to-reduce-entry-level-hiring-needs)
- Gartner projection cited by SupplyChainBrain: 60% of supply chain disruptions resolved without human intervention by 2031 (forecast, not result) — [SupplyChainBrain](https://www.supplychainbrain.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/blog/15882228/how-supply-chains-can-responsibly-implement-agentic-ai-at-scale)
- project44 claims "95% of AI agent pilots fail in supply chains" but that is the MIT cross-industry gen AI figure relabeled; it attributes failure to missing context, weak data, poor workflow integration (vendor marketing) — [project44](https://www.project44.com/blog/why-95-percent-of-ai-agent-pilots-fail-in-supply-chains/)
- SCMR: leaders are resetting after underperforming agent pilots toward decision ownership, governance, data integrity and constrained, task-level autonomy — [SCMR](https://www.scmr.com/article/after-ai-agent-pilots-underperformed-resetting-supply-chain-automation-for-operational-impact)
- Governance guidance: tier use cases by risk, keep immutable audit trails for high-impact decisions; EU AI Act requires documentation and human oversight for high-risk systems — [SupplyChainBrain](https://www.supplychainbrain.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/blog/15882228/how-supply-chains-can-responsibly-implement-agentic-ai-at-scale)
- Vendors (project44, FourKites, Sanka) claim production exception agents with audit logging; no independent outcome data — [project44](https://www.project44.com/es/?p=80631); [FourKites/ABI](https://abiresearch.com/market-research/insight/7785828-fourkites-announce-agentic-ai-innovationsi)

### Inferences
- Failure modes reported are mainly integration, data quality, silo-local optimization and ownership, rather than documented hallucination incidents.

### Gaps
- No independent hallucination-rate or incident data for production supply chain agents found.
- No named Fortune 500 case study with audited agentic ROI located; earnings-call and Supply Chain Dive/FreightWaves deep reads not completed (fetch blocked).

## 4. Which unmet needs have no good product today?

### Takeaway
No source states this directly; the following is inference from the gaps above, labeled as such.

### Cited Findings
- Multi-tier visibility is blocked by lack of data (47% of US manufacturers per D&B) — [D&B](https://www.dnb.com/en-us/blog/supplier-risk/building-resilience-supply-chain-risk-insights-for-2026.html)
- Claims and carrier disputes automation is only recently productized, by startups/vendors — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/freight-billing-gets-ai-boost-as-kleinschmidt-backs-upwell)

### Inferences
- Candidate unmet needs: (a) cross-enterprise, multi-tier data capture (a data-sharing problem, not an AI one); (b) auditable agent governance/provenance layer spanning ERP/TMS/WMS; (c) planning that incorporates tariff scenarios and decision ownership across functions; (d) outcome measurement/ROI attribution (55% unclear on returns); (e) claims evidence assembly end to end. These are hypotheses, not verified market gaps.

### Gaps
- No analyst source naming "no good product" categories; Gartner Hype Cycle abstracts not read.
