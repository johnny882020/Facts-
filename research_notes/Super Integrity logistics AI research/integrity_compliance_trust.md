## Regulation: deadlines and penalty/cost data (UFLPA, CSDDD, DPP/ESPR, CBAM, EU AI Act, EUDR, DSCSA, CTPAT, export controls)

### Takeaway
The 2026-2029 EU calendar was pushed right by "omnibus" legislation (CSDDD to 2029, EUDR to Dec 2026, AI Act high-risk to Dec 2027/Aug 2028), while US/EU border-enforcement regimes (UFLPA entity list, CBAM certificates, BIS affiliates rule) are tightening on near dates. Hard compliance-cost numbers are scarce; penalty and price points are what is citable.

### Inferences
- Repeated EU delays shift cost from "build now" to "uncertain target", but data-collection needs (supplier-level origin, emissions, ownership) are common across UFLPA, CBAM, EUDR, CSDDD, DPP and export-control ownership screening, so a shared supplier-provenance data layer is the durable need.
- The 19 non-Xinjiang entity additions and 50% ownership screening both require multi-tier ownership/graph data, which most ERPs do not hold.
- CBAM default-value markups create a direct euro incentive to obtain verified supplier data (inference from the markup schedule).

## Data integrity: master data, conflicting shipment/ETA data, document fraud, cargo theft and freight fraud

### Takeaway
Cargo theft value jumped sharply in 2025 with strategic/identity-based fraud rising; document fraud is well documented qualitatively but widely repeated loss statistics lack primary sources. Gartner's master-data cost figure is a generic, self-reported benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- Verisk CargoNet: ~US$725M estimated 2025 US/Canada cargo theft losses, +60% vs 2024; average value per theft $273,990 vs $202,364 (+36%); supply-chain crime events flat (3,594 vs 3,607); confirmed cargo theft incidents +18% (2,243 to 2,646); California 1,218 incidents — [Verisk](https://www.verisk.com/company/newsroom/cargo-theft-losses-surge-to-estimated-$725-million-in-2025-verisk-cargonet-analysis-reveals/); [CCJ](https://www.ccjdigital.com/regulations/article/15815405/cargo-theft-activity-flat-losses-surged-in-2025-cargonet); [Aftermarket News](https://www.aftermarketnews.com/cargo-theft-losses-surge-to-725m-in-2025/)
- CargoNet warns of fraudulent takeovers/acquisitions of existing carriers with strong load histories — [Truckinginfo](https://www.truckinginfo.com/news/cargo-theft-losses-jump-60-in-2025-as-criminals-target-higher-value-freight)
- Origin fraud: White House labeled false routing/origin claims the "Great Transshipment Scam" in 2026; schemes use inaccurate certificates of origin, invoices, packing lists, bills of lading — [MalwareTips summary](https://malwaretips.com/blogs/?p=417133) (low-quality source; VERIFY primary)
- B/L fraud: easily edited via PDF tools; insider collusion common; IMB data flagged Chinese steel as most common commodity in suspect B/Ls — [Resistant AI](https://resistant.ai/blog/how-to-spot-fake-bills-of-lading); [GTR](https://www.gtreview.com/news/global/banks-warned-over-collusion-in-trade-documents-fraud/)
- ICC published a Vessel Checking Guide (Jul 2026) on using B/L data to spot sanctions evasion/TBML — [ICC](https://iccwbo.org/news-publications/guide/financial-crime-risk-controls-vessel-checking/); ICC UK says lenders are hampered by lack of electronic transferable documents — [GTR](https://www.gtreview.com/?p=101703)
- ICC 2022 estimate: fraud-related financier losses up to $2.5B — [Insurance Journal](https://insurancejournal.com/news/international/2023/10/04/742932.htm)
- Gartner: poor data quality averages $12.9M/year (2020 survey of 154 orgs, self-estimated); 59% of orgs do not measure data quality; silos/inconsistencies the hardest problem — [DataTechVibe](https://datatechvibe.com/?p=21661); [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/data-analytics/topics/data-quality)
- Unverified: "$50B annual B/L losses / 40% of cases" figures on [Freightamigo](https://www.freightamigo.com/en/blog/trade-finance/the-challenge-of-fraud-in-trade-financing-and-how-to-prevent-it/) attributed to ICC without primary source — do not use.

### Inferences
- Flat incident counts with +60% value suggests concentration on higher-value loads and organised/identity-based methods; counterparty identity verification (carrier, broker, consignee) is the control point.
- $12.9M is not supply-chain specific; no supply-chain-specific master-data cost number was found.

### Gaps
- CargoNet breakdown of fictitious pickup/strategic theft counts; TAPA EMEA 2025/2026 data; FBI/other freight-fraud losses: not retrieved.
- Quantified cost of conflicting ETA/shipment data across partners (no reliable study found).

## AI supply chain integrity: model/dataset provenance, AI-BOM, prompt injection, data poisoning, agent risk

### Takeaway
Standards exist (CycloneDX ML-BOM, SPDX 3.0 AI profile, OWASP LLM03/LLM04 and Agentic Top 10), but tooling and adoption lag, AI-BOMs only record rather than prevent poisoning, and indirect prompt injection and MCP tool poisoning are now observed in the wild per vendor/CSA research.

### Cited Findings
- OWASP LLM Top 10 2025: supply chain LLM03, data/model poisoning LLM04; guidance favors signed inventory, CycloneDX — [Opcito summary](https://www.opcito.com/blogs/ai-supply-chain-security-risk); [Endor Labs](https://www.endorlabs.com/learn/what-is-aibom)
- CycloneDX ML-BOM since v1.5, reportedly Ecma-424; version of SPDX/CycloneDX AI support differs across sources (VERIFY) — [SentinelOne](https://www.sentinelone.com/cybersecurity-101/data-and-ai/ai-bill-of-materials-aibom/); [Tianpan](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-05-02-ai-bill-of-materials-aibom-when-procurement-asks)
- ML-BOM adoption/tooling significantly behind SBOM; AIBOM does not stop poisoning that occurs at training — [NHIMG](https://nhimg.org/ai-supply-chain-ai-bom-guide); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/security/seven-steps-to-ai-supply-chain-visibility)
- 6.5x increase in malicious models on Hugging Face in 2024 (one report, unnamed in my results) — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/security/seven-steps-to-ai-supply-chain-visibility) (VERIFY)
- OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications published 9 Dec 2025; ASI01 Agent Goal Hijack, ASI04 supply chain (compromised MCP servers, plugins, sub-agents) — [Koi](https://koi.ai/blog/owasp-agentic-ai-top-10-a-practical-security-guide); [Cycode](https://cycode.com/?p=12786)
- CSA research note (Apr 2026): indirect prompt injection now operational/in the wild — [CSA Labs](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-indirect-prompt-injection-in-the-wild-2026/)
- MCP tool poisoning first disclosed by Invariant Labs Apr 2025; postmark-mcp BCC incident; LiteLLM breach (dates conflict: 24 Mar vs 3 Apr 2026) — [SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/prompt-injection-in-production-the-2026-state-of-the-industrial-ai-attack); [Meta-Intelligence](https://meta-intelligence.tech/en/insight-ai-agent-security) (vendor blogs; VERIFY)

### Inferences
- Agents reading shipment documents, carrier emails, EDI notes or supplier portals ingest attacker-controllable text (e.g., a forged B/L PDF or a fake carrier email), so prompt-injection exposure maps directly onto the document-fraud problem above (inference).
- Provenance must be captured at training/ingestion time; retrofitting is unreliable (per cited sources), so enterprises buying third-party logistics AI have little way to verify.

### Gaps
- No supply-chain/logistics-specific agent incident with a quantified loss found.
- NIST AI RMF/SP 800-53 overlays, ISO/IEC 42001 provenance requirements not searched.
- Primary OWASP and CycloneDX spec pages not opened.

## Auditability: what AI-driven decisions must log (finance/legal/regulators)

### Takeaway
EU AI Act Arts. 12/19/26(6) require automatic event logs retained at least six months for high-risk systems; broader finance/legal logging expectations (SOX, tariff recordkeeping) were not researched, so the list below is mostly an inference.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 12 mandates automatic logging capability but sets no retention period; Art. 19 sets minimum six months retention for providers, Art. 26(6) the same for deployers — [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/04/16/eu-ai-act-logging-requirements/); [AI Act Service Desk Art. 19](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-19); [OpenMetal](https://openmetal.io/resources/blog/what-the-eu-ai-acts-article-12-requires-from-your-log-infrastructure/)
- One source says Art. 12/19 apply from 2 Aug 2026; this predates the Omnibus delay to Dec 2027/Aug 2028 for high-risk and is likely outdated — VERIFY.
- Enterprise guidance: log full context at decision points (inputs, tool calls, outputs) for agents — [AIAgentRank](https://aiagentrank.io/blog/ai-agent-security-2026) (vendor)

### Inferences
- Minimum useful audit record for an AI-made logistics/finance decision: model + version/hash, prompt/config, input data and source provenance (document IDs, hashes), tool/API calls, retrieved context, output, confidence, human approver/override, timestamp, downstream action. This is my synthesis, not drawn from a regulator checklist.
- Customs-related decisions (HTS classification, origin, valuation) carry importer reasonable-care duties, so AI-assisted declarations need traceable evidence; not sourced here.

### Gaps
- SOX/PCAOB, SEC, CBP recordkeeping (19 USC 1508, 5 years) requirements for AI decisions not researched.
- ISO/IEC 42001, NIST AI RMF logging expectations not researched.
