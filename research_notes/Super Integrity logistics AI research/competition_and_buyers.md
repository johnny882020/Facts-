# Competition and Fortune 500 Buyer Requirements for Supply Chain / Logistics Software (as of Nov 2026)

Method note: only ~15 tool calls were available. Search results came back as tool-summarized snippets; freightwaves.com could not be fetched directly (host not in the session's allowed network domains), so FreightWaves facts rest on search-result summaries. Funding and valuation figures are third-party and often conflict. Treat anything marked "estimate" as unverified.

## 1. Vendors, funding/valuation, what each does and does not do

### Takeaway
Visibility (project44, FourKites), risk/mapping (Resilinc, Everstream, Altana, Overhaul, Sourcemap), audit (Cass, Trax, nVision), D&D point tools (Vizion, Portcast, BlueCargo) and tariff tools (Zonos, Avalara, Flexport) each own one slice. Funding data is noisy: project44 about $2.7B, FourKites last primary round $1.7B (2024) with much lower secondary marks, Altana $1B (2024). I found no evidence of a single product that unifies freight money (audit/D&D claims), shipment proof, and customs/tariff compliance. That is an inference, not a sourced fact.

## 2. Gaps no incumbent fills (unified money + proof + compliance)

### Takeaway
No source I found describes a product combining freight-invoice recovery, shipment evidence, and customs/tariff compliance. Evidence is by absence and by vendor category boundaries.

### Cited Findings
- FAP is described as moving toward broader "transportation spend management", but coverage is of invoices and cost types, not shipment evidence — [Inbound Logistics](https://www.inboundlogistics.com/articles/beyond-the-freight-bill-audit/)
- D&D tools are point solutions combining tracking data with fee tracking (Vizion+Northbound, BlueCargo) — [StartupRise](https://startuprise.co.uk/northbound-vizion-reducing-dd-with-real-time-visibility/); [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/bluecargo-raises-11m-to-bring-visibility-to-drayage-fees)
- FMC D&D rule (46 CFR 541): invoices missing required minimum information mean the billed party is not obligated to pay (541.5); invoices due within 30 days of the last incurred charge (541.7, verify exact trigger); rule effective May 28, 2024; the ban on billing both consignee and contracting party (541.4) was set aside by the D.C. Circuit in 2025 and removed by the FMC. This creates a compliance-driven reason to verify invoices against evidence. — [Federal Register 2024](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-02-26/pdf/2024-02926.pdf); [Federal Register 2025](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2025-23920.pdf); [Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/a-whole-new-world-fmc-issues-final-rule-on-demurrage-and-detention-billing-requirements/pdf/)
- Tariff tools split between checkout speed (Zonos) and classification with human review (Avalara) — see Section 1.

### Inferences
- The white space is the evidentiary link: tying a charge (invoice, D&D, duty) to timestamped proof (container events, gate records, rulings) and to the rule it must satisfy (FMC 541, HTS). Not validated with buyers.

### Gaps
- No analyst market guide found that defines this category; no buyer interviews.

## 3. How Fortune 500 buys: security, integrations, cycle, pricing, pilots

### Takeaway
SOC 2 Type II is the near-universal gate; SSO/SAML/OIDC, SCIM, exportable audit logs and data-residency answers recur in questionnaires. Cycles are commonly cited at 6-18 months. Freight audit is traditionally gain-share/contingency or hybrid; SaaS-style fees are being pushed by challengers. Most sourcing is vendor blogs, not procurement primary sources.

### Cited Findings
- SOC 2 Type II treated as the compliance floor; Type II tests operating effectiveness over a window typically 6-12 months — [Security Boulevard](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/04/9-critical-security-questionnaire-items-that-stall-enterprise-saas-deals/); [SSOJet](https://ssojet.com/blog/enterprise-security-questionnaire-saas)
- US enterprises often anchor on SOC 2; many global enterprises expect ISO 27001 (plus SoA, EU-specific questionnaires for European banks) — [WorkOS](https://workos.com/blog/enterprise-security-questionnaire-identity); [SSOJet](https://ssojet.com/blog/what-makes-enterprise-authentication-enterprise-ready)
- Enterprise-ready checklist cited: SAML + OIDC + SCIM, written 99.99% uptime, exportable audit logs, self-serve admin portal, enforceable MFA; questionnaires ask who authenticated, changed roles, connected an IdP, exported data; also audit-log retention, data residency, sub-processor lists, GDPR breach notification — [eSecurityPlanet](https://www.esecurityplanet.com/access-management/ready-for-the-enterprise-sso-request-an-identity-checklist-for-growing-saas-teams/); [WorkOS](https://workos.com/blog/enterprise-security-questionnaire-identity); [Security Boulevard](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/04/9-critical-security-questionnaire-items-that-stall-enterprise-saas-deals/)
- Questionnaire formats include CAIQ — [Bastion](https://bastion.tech/blog/what-is-caiq-consensus-assessments-initiative-questionnaire-saas)
- EDI sets: 204 load tender (990 response), 214 shipment status, 210 freight invoice, 856 ASN, 997 acknowledgment, 820 payment; transport via AS2 or FTP. One vendor mislabels 210; use implementation guides — [TrueCommerce](https://truecommerce.com/edi-transaction-codes); [Cleo](https://www.cleo.com/edi-codes/logistics); [TrueCommerce trucking](https://www.truecommerce.com/en-gb/blog/edi-for-trucking/)
- Sales cycle: estimates 6-18+ months for enterprise SaaS; a Gartner attribution for Fortune 500 procurement is unverified; Forrester cited 6.4 months (2015) to 9.3 months (2023), unverified; one practitioner says Fortune 500 procurement takes ~2-3 months after legal signs off; a six-month POC can become the default — [Pedowitz Group](https://www.pedowitzgroup.com/how-long-are-enterprise-software-journeys); [Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/how-to-navigate-the-fortune-500-procurement-process-for-enterprise-saas-vendors); [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/cope-long-sales-cycles); [Substack](https://andrewjturner.substack.com/p/the-9-month-reality-of-enterprise)
- FAP pricing: gain share typically 25-50% of identified savings (one source); hybrid per-invoice fee plus gain share described as most common; parcel-audit contingency 8-15% (tiered 10%/12%/15%); outsourced audit savings about 2% of freight bill in year one (one source) or 3-5% (another) — vendor/consultancy claims — [Racklify](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/inbound-freight-audit-costs-savings-and-who-pays-pricing-models-and-roi/); [ShipWare](https://shipware.com/blog/category/news/invoice-auditing/page/4); [Lojistic](https://lojistic.com/blog/freight-audit-pricing-and-benefits); [MMH](https://www.mmh.com/article/average_savings_found_after_a_freight_bill_audit)

### Inferences
- A day-0 MVP can plausibly defer SOC 2 Type II itself (observation window) but should have SOC 2 Type I or a readiness plan, SSO/SAML, RBAC, audit logs, and a documented subprocessor/data-residency posture. Day-0 gain-share on a read-only pilot lowers procurement friction. These are my recommendations, not sourced.
- Because pilots are the stretch point, a time-boxed, scoped pilot on a limited lane/carrier set is likely safer.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source on SAP/Oracle/Blue Yonder TMS/WMS integration requirements (only one SAP Business One mention); no F500 data-residency specifics; no supply-chain-specific sales cycle benchmark; no primary procurement documents.

## 4. Legitimate public data sources for a day-0 MVP

### Takeaway
Good public options: USITC HTS, Census trade API, USITC DataWeb, FMC tariff lists and the FR/CFR text for rules. CBP ACE is filer/portal-based, not an open API; port data (Port of LA) lacks documented API terms; AIS commercial use needs license review.

### Cited Findings
- FMC: carriers must publish tariffs openly and give free access to tariff systems; service contracts are filed confidentially, only Statements of Essential Terms are public; FMC lists VOCC/NVOCC tariffs and service contract statistics — [FMC (archived)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20201218015749/https://www.fmc.gov/resources-services/vessel-operating-common-carriers); [FMC databases (archived)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20201218012353/https://www.fmc.gov/databases-services); [46 CFR 530](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2023-title46-vol9/pdf/CFR-2023-title46-vol9-sec530-1.pdf). Scraping terms for individual carrier tariff sites must be checked.
- CBP ACE: Secure Data Portal is free but needs an account; electronic filing requires testing with CBP; developer portal exists; open-source testing tools planned (2024, a plan only) — [CBP ACE](https://cbp.gov/ace); [CBP developers](https://cbp.gov/document/guidance/ace-electronic-truck-manifest-software-developers); [ITT](https://internationaltradetoday.com/article/2024/03/28/testing-of-new-ace-20-capabilities-ramps-up-funding-top-cbp-concern-2403280042)
- USITC HTS: free public data (sources conflict on key need). USITC DataWeb API needs free registration and a key valid 6 months. Census trade API requires a free key — [USITC DataWeb](https://usitc.gov/applications/dataweb/api/dataweb_query_api.html); [USITC data](https://www.usitc.gov/data/index.htm); [Census](https://cdn.www.census.gov/data/developers/data-sets/international-trade.html)
- Port of LA: Port Optimizer (with Wabtec) is a stakeholder platform; The Signal gives a three-week look-ahead; no documented public API found — [Port of LA](https://portoflosangeles.org/references/news_090320_Signal); [Port Tech](https://porttechnology.org/news/port-of-los-angeles-extends-port-optimizer-coverage-to-all-container-terminals)
- MarineTraffic: free map is basic; API/premium is under legally binding terms; I did not retrieve the terms — [Wikipedia mirror/Kpler help](https://help.kpler.com/en/articles/9552867)

### Inferences
- Rule-and-tariff reference data (CFR, HTS) is the safest day-0 content; shipment evidence should come from customer-provided data (EDI 214, carrier APIs under customer credentials, BL/invoice documents) rather than scraped sources.

### Gaps
- Ocean carrier API terms (Maersk, MSC, etc.), terminal API access, AIS licensing, and CBP data usable without being a broker were not researched.

## 5. Enterprise design expectations and pain points

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin. The clearest signals are G2 complaints about data accuracy for smaller carriers, report export friction and implementation complexity.

### Cited Findings
- Reviewers cite inaccurate ETAs/visibility gaps, inconsistent small-carrier tracking, too many clicks for a basic datasheet, and Excel download hassle (project44) — [G2](https://www.g2.com/compare/project44-vs-fourkites); [G2 reviews](https://g2.com/products/project44/reviews)
- Complex setup/implementation cited as a con; robust APIs as a pro — [SaaSworthy](https://www.saasworthy.com/product/project44/reviews)
- Contingency terms need scrutiny for exclusions and minimums, and buyers are advised to require shipment-level detail to audit savings calculations — [ShipWare](https://shipware.com/blog/category/news/invoice-auditing/page/4); [Lojistic](https://lojistic.com/blog/freight-audit-pricing-and-benefits)

### Inferences
- Buyers likely want line-level, exportable, auditable evidence for every claimed dollar, with simple CSV/Excel export and fast onboarding. This is inferred from the above, not directly sourced.

### Gaps
- No usability studies, Gartner Peer Insights, or interview-based sources; legacy FAP portal complaints (Cass/Trax) not retrieved.
