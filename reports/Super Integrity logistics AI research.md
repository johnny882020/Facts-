# Super Integrity Turns Shipment Evidence Into Recovered Cash

**Super Integrity should be built as one evidence-backed "claims engine": it ties every charge a Fortune 500 shipper pays (duty, ocean demurrage and detention, freight accessorials) to timestamped proof and to the rule that governs it, then files the dispute or refund claim.** The three ideas that combine most naturally are (1) tariff and customs recovery and compliance, led by IEEPA refunds and then Section 232/301 stacking and classification checks; (2) ocean demurrage/detention (D&D) and freight-invoice validation against contract and FMC billing rules; and (3) a shared provenance and decision-audit layer (shipment events, document hashes, multi-tier origin and ownership, and a log of every AI or human decision). The three share one data model: Charge, Evidence, Rule, Claim, Decision. The market evidence supports the direction but is weaker than it looks. No independent study shows a payback under six months for any category, and nearly all savings percentages come from vendors. Large pools do exist: IEEPA duties of roughly $166B were collected (one source) and refunds are in progress ([Passport Global](https://passportglobal.com/blog/ieepa-tariffs-the-supreme-court-an-ongoing-guide-for-importers/)); nine ocean carriers collected about $15.4B in D&D over five years ([FMC](https://www.fmc.gov/?p=4790)). AI in logistics is also under-delivering, with only about 13% of logistics providers reporting measurable financial impact ([BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/why-ai-isnt-delivering-roi-logistics)). That gap favors a narrow, auditable, cash-denominated product over a general agent platform. Several load-bearing facts are unresolved as of 12 Nov 2026, notably whether the BIS 50% Affiliates Rule suspension ended on 9 Nov, the EUDR date, and the exact trigger of the FMC 30-day invoice rule. They are listed in the verification gate below, and the MVP must not depend on them.

## The AI industry's physical supply chain is bottlenecked, stolen from, and slipping

The binding constraints on AI buildout in 2026 are memory, packaging, optics and grid-side electrical gear, and they rotate. Micron reported HBM for 2026 sold out under multi-year agreements, with about 75% of 2027 output already committed ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/micron-record-quarter-memory-shortage-2028)). Nvidia's supply and capacity commitments were **$119B at 26 Apr 2026** per its 10-Q ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000052/nvda-20260426.htm)), and a reported **$279B** by July is secondary only ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/nvidias-supply-commitments-more-doubled-279-billion-heres-what-it-does-stock)). Large prepayments concentrate allocation toward the biggest buyers, which is an inference from the filings. Power gear is slower still: generator step-up transformer lead times exceeded 160 weeks in Q1 2026 versus 143 weeks on average in 2024 ([Kitco/Reuters](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-07-09/us-power-companies-scramble-secure-equipment-surging-data-center)), and 72% of operators in the Uptime 2026 survey worry about supply chain disruption ([Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/ai-data-centers/ai-drives-data-center-uncertainty-in-uptime-s-2026-survey)). Sightline estimates 30-50% of 2026 data-center capacity may slip to 2027 or later, a denominator SemiAnalysis disputes ([Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/4201941/up-to-50-of-data-center-capacity-slated-for-2026-could-be-delayed.html)). Cost-of-delay figures are modeled rather than observed, ranging from roughly $150M to $900M per gigawatt-month ([Occam Edge](https://occamedge.substack.com/p/confronting-the-datacenter-delay)). The one hard datapoint is QTS paying **$55.1M to accelerate a project four months**, as reported from a FERC filing ([Ambition Capital](https://ambitioncapital.substack.com/p/time-to-power)).

The shipment-handling problem is better documented because crime and enforcement leave records. Verisk CargoNet estimates **$725M** of US cargo theft in 2025, up 60%, with average value per theft up 36% to $273,990 while event counts stayed flat ([Verisk](https://www.verisk.com/company/newsroom/cargo-theft-losses-surge-to-estimated-%24725-million-in-2025-verisk-cargonet-analysis-reveals/)). Flat counts with rising value point to targeting of higher-value loads and identity-based methods such as carrier takeovers ([Truckinginfo](https://www.truckinginfo.com/news/cargo-theft-losses-jump-60-in-2025-as-criminals-target-higher-value-freight)). AI servers are now a named target, with individual heists reported between roughly $7M and $38M ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/cargo-thieves-rammed-security-escorts-to-hijack-ai-hardware-shipments-in-california), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/03/organized-crime-cargo-theft-economy-ai-infrastructure-data-center-black-market/)). Smuggling is the mirror image: the Hao Global case involved about $160M of H100/H200 GPUs bound for China ([Washington Times](https://m.washingtontimes.com/news/2025/dec/9/doj-operation-busts-160-million-network-smuggling-nvidia-chips-china/)), and FDD argues the March 2026 cases show the limits of industry self-policing ([FDD](https://www.fdd.org/analysis/2026/03/20/exposure-of-major-chinese-linked-chip-smuggling-operations-shows-limits-of-industry-self-policing/)). Counterfeit evidence is thin for data-center parts; documented fakes are mostly consumer RTX cards ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/scam-nvidia-rtx-4090-uses-a-relabeled-rtx-3080-ti-gpu-counterfeit-memory-chips)). The unmet needs here (serial-level chain of custody from chip to rack, cross-vendor energization sequencing, secondary-market provenance) are my inferences, not findings from a vendor-landscape review. They matter to Super Integrity mainly as a later vertical for the same provenance layer, because the AI-hardware buyer pool is small and its pain is mostly capital delay, not invoice leakage.

## AI in logistics stalls on integration, ownership and proof, not on model quality

Every large survey in the notes agrees that adoption is wide and scaled value is narrow. Gartner finds only 17% of supply chain organizations pursuing an immediate AI-driven workflow redesign ([Gartner](https://gcomdr.pdo.aws.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-06-gartner-survey-shows-ai-is-not-driving-supply-chain-operating-model-transformation)); BCG reports about **13% of logistics providers with measurable financial impact** and roughly one in ten with AI embedded in core operations ([BCG](https://www.bcg.com/press/27march2026-ai-expectations-rise-in-logistics-scaled-adoption-remains-limited)); and 55% of supply chain chiefs are unclear about their AI returns ([Supply Chain 24/7](https://www.supplychain247.com/article/gartner-supply-chain-chiefs-unclear-ai-investment-returns/radial)). The MIT NANDA 95% figure for gen-AI pilots is cross-industry, and project44 relabels it as a supply chain statistic ([project44](https://www.project44.com/blog/why-95-percent-of-ai-agent-pilots-fail-in-supply-chains/)), so the Super Integrity pitch should not quote it as logistics evidence. Deloitte's contrary 84% ROI figure reflects a different sample and definition of value ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/digital-transformation/ai-tech-investment-roi.html)). The reported blockers are fragmented data, legacy integration, unclear decision ownership and silo-local optimization ([SCMR](https://www.scmr.com/article/after-ai-agent-pilots-underperformed-resetting-supply-chain-automation-for-operational-impact)). Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be canceled by end-2027 ([SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/agentic-ai/gartner-more-than-40-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-in-next-few-years/)).

The use cases that work have a bounded decision, structured data and a cash outcome. The cleanest example is freight invoice exceptions: Worldwide Express reports about 14% of LTL invoices failing three-way match, and AI agents cutting active exceptions by 75% and releasing a one-time $10M of cash, all self-reported ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/how-worldwide-express-automated-freight-exceptions-with-amazon-bedrock/)). Visibility is the self-reported gap, and it is worst beyond Tier 1: only 6% of firms report full Tier 2/3 visibility ([Hellenic Shipping News](https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/achilles-global-survey-reveals-major-visibility-gaps-across-supply-chains-as-regulatory-pressure-drives-risk-management-priorities/)), and 47% of US manufacturers cite lack of data as the blocker ([D&B](https://www.dnb.com/en-us/blog/supplier-risk/building-resilience-supply-chain-risk-insights-for-2026.html)). The 16% versus 97% spread on end-to-end visibility ([Supply Chain 24/7](https://www.supplychain247.com/article/most-companies-say-their-supply-chains-work-the-data-says-otherwise/software); [FedEx](https://digital-blog.fedex.com/3-key-insights-from-the-future-of-logistics-intelligence-report)) is a definitional gap, not a measurement error, and it tells us to define "proof" per charge rather than promise "visibility." No named Fortune 500 case with audited agentic ROI exists in the notes, and no independent hallucination or incident data for production supply chain agents was found. Two security points follow from the integrity theme. Agents that read forged bills of lading, carrier emails or supplier portals ingest attacker-controlled text, and indirect prompt injection is now reported operating in the wild ([CSA Labs](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-indirect-prompt-injection-in-the-wild-2026/)); the mapping to document fraud is my inference. And the EU AI Act's logging duties (Art. 19, six-month minimum retention) apply to high-risk systems after the Omnibus delay to Dec 2027 or Aug 2028 ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/04/16/eu-ai-act-logging-requirements/); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines/)), so audit logging is a buyer expectation and a differentiator, not yet a hard mandate for this product.

The ordering turns on one judgment: ideas 1 and 2 are the ones where a dollar can be tied to a document, a timestamp and a written rule, and idea 3 is what makes that tie auditable. The retailer-deduction pool may be larger in nominal terms, but its recoverable share is unestablished and the category already has dedicated vendors, so it ranks as the first expansion rather than a launch wedge. A steelman for dropping idea 3 as a "pick" is that buyers purchase recovered dollars, not provenance, and that Altana and project44 already sell visibility; the answer is that the notes found no product linking charge, proof and rule in one chain, but that is absence of evidence, not a validated gap. The shipper-side pain that supports the combination is the FMC rule itself: invoices missing required minimum information are not payable (541.5), which converts evidence quality directly into avoided payments ([Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/a-whole-new-world-fmc-issues-final-rule-on-demurrage-and-detention-billing-requirements/pdf/)).

## How the three ideas become one product and one data model

The product is a single workflow with three surfaces. A shipper connects customs entry data, carrier invoices and shipment documents. Super Integrity extracts each charge, attaches the evidence that supports or contradicts it, evaluates it against the governing rule, and produces a claim packet (a refund request, a D&D dispute or an accessorial challenge) with a human approver. Duty recovery, D&D disputes and provenance are not three modules. They are three rule books run against the same graph, so adding a fourth (retailer deductions, CBAM default-value avoidance) means adding rules and connectors, not a new schema.

The data model has six core entities and two cross-cutting properties. A Party is a legal entity with ownership edges (supplier, carrier, broker, consignee), which supports the multi-tier and 50%-ownership screening the notes identify as the common need across UFLPA, BIS and CBAM. A Consignment groups shipments, containers and, for AI hardware later, serialized units. An Event is a timestamped custody or status fact (gate-out, vessel discharge, entry filed, EDI 214 status) with its source and confidence. A Document is an immutable file with a hash, type and extracted fields (bill of lading, commercial invoice, carrier invoice, entry summary, EDI 210/214). A Charge is any money line: a duty line on an entry, a D&D invoice line, an accessorial. A Rule is a versioned, effective-dated clause (an HTS rate and its Section 232/301 stacking, FMC 541.5 minimum-information elements and 541.7 timing, a contract rate, a CAPE eligibility condition). Charge, Event, Document and Rule connect through Evidence links that record whether a document or event supports, contradicts or is missing for a charge under a rule. Claims reference the charges and Evidence they depend on and carry status through filing to cash recovered. Decisions record every AI extraction, rule evaluation and human override. Two properties apply everywhere: every fact is bitemporal (when it was true versus when we learned it), because rules change retroactively and refunds depend on past status; and every AI step writes a decision record with the minimum audit content I propose (model and version, prompt or config, input document hashes, tool calls, output, confidence, approver, timestamp, downstream action). That audit list is my synthesis, not a regulator checklist ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/04/16/eu-ai-act-logging-requirements/) covers only the EU logging duty).

The design rule that follows from the agent-failure evidence is that models extract and propose, while a deterministic rule engine decides and a human approves anything that moves money or goes to a regulator. This answers the reported failure modes (unclear decision ownership, hallucination exposure, weak audit trails) and the prompt-injection risk from hostile documents. It also fits the Remio observation that software can extract an invoice correctly yet misjudge whether the charge belongs, which is a rule-and-evidence problem, not an extraction problem.

## Day-0 MVP: product implications

The MVP should prove one claim: for a named Fortune 500 shipper, Super Integrity finds dollars that are tied to documents and rules, and the customer's controller can audit every one. Four implications follow.

First, scope to two wedges on the shared model. Wedge one is IEEPA refund eligibility and tracking from customer entry-summary exports, because it has the largest identifiable pool and a closing window (CAPE Phase 3 opened 6 Oct 2026; rules for finally liquidated entries are contested). Verify the process first, since the notes could not read CBP's own pages. Wedge two is ocean D&D and accessorial invoice validation: check each charge for the FMC 541.5 minimum-information elements, the 30-day timing rule once its trigger is confirmed, and the contract rate, using bills of lading, container events and invoices the customer supplies. Everything else (classification review, Section 232/301 stacking, retailer deductions, CBAM, UFLPA screening) stays on the roadmap as rule packs on the same model.

Second, source evidence the safe way. Load public rule text (CFR, Federal Register, USITC HTS) as versioned, effective-dated Rules, and take shipment proof from customer-provided EDI 214/210, carrier APIs under the customer's credentials, and uploaded BL and invoice documents. Do not scrape carrier or port sites, buy AIS, or claim ACE access until terms are reviewed.

Third, build the enterprise gate in from day 0 but sequence it. The notes treat SOC 2 Type II as the near-universal floor, with SSO/SAML, RBAC and exportable audit logs recurring in questionnaires ([Security Boulevard](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/04/9-critical-security-questionnaire-items-that-stall-enterprise-saas-deals/)). Since Type II needs a 6-12 month observation window, the MVP should ship with SSO, RBAC, immutable decision logs and a subprocessor and data-residency sheet, and begin the SOC 2 process immediately (my recommendation, not sourced). Run pilots as a time-boxed, read-only engagement on a bounded set of lanes and carriers. Price on gain share first to lower procurement friction, but note Loop's argument that gain-share vendors profit from persistent errors ([Loop](https://loop.com/article/gain-share-pricing-is-broken)); a platform fee plus a success fee is the safer long-run structure. Both are untested hypotheses.

Fourth, make trust the feature. Every dollar shown carries a one-click evidence packet (documents, events, rule text, version, decision record) that exports to CSV or Excel, because buyers complain about report friction and want shipment-level detail to audit savings claims ([ShipWare](https://shipware.com/blog/category/news/invoice-auditing/page/4); [G2](https://www.g2.com/compare/project44-vs-fourkites)). Use models only for extraction and drafting; keep the rule engine deterministic; treat all inbound documents as untrusted input; require human approval before any filing. Track one success metric from the first pilot: recovered or avoided dollars with a complete evidence packet, measured against an agreed baseline, since no independent payback benchmark exists.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the question. The AI industry's own logistics problems are real but mostly capital-delay problems for a small buyer set, while AI in logistics fails for lack of integration, ownership and proof. The common fix is evidence, not another agent. Super Integrity's defensible position is the link between a charge, its proof and its governing rule, which turns a vague "integrity" promise into dollars a controller can audit. The same link is what the unresolved regulatory calendar (BIS, EUDR, FMC timing, the CAPE process) will keep changing, so a versioned, effective-dated rule layer is both the moat and the main operational risk.

The weakest part of the case is the numbers. Recovery rates, payback periods and the "no incumbent does this" claim have no independent support in the notes, and the largest single pool, IEEPA refunds, is one-off and already contested by brokers and law firms. The first pilot should therefore be treated as the measurement exercise that produces Super Integrity's first real benchmark, and the verification gate above should be cleared before any figure from this report reaches a customer.
