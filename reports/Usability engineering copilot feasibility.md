# Consistency checking makes the usability copilot viable

A part-time solo founder can build an AI copilot for IEC 62366-1 / FDA human factors (HF) documentation into a real niche business. Reaching $1M ARR is possible, but realistically it takes 5–7 years part-time, or 3–4 years if the founder goes full-time around year two. Those timelines are estimates, not sourced figures. Demand is documented. FDA's final HF content guidance took effect on **August 1, 2026** with a new report structure tied to eSTAR, so every US submitter has to rework templates ([NSF](https://www.nsf.org/life-science-regulatory-news/fda-issues-final-guidance-on-human-factors-information-in-medical-device-submissions)). Notified bodies name usability handled separately from risk management as **"one of the most frequently seen problems"** ([TÜV NORD](https://www.tuv-nord.com/pl/en/uslugi/medical-devices/mdr-knowledge-hub/usability-engineering-of-non-active-medical-devices-in-light-of-the-mdr/)). No AI-native, usability-specific competitor was found. Incumbents are close, though: Greenlight Guru already mines adverse-event data for hazards, and OpenRegulatory/Formwork sells IEC 62366 templates "with AI assistance" inside a €99–499/month eQMS ([OpenRegulatory](https://openregulatory.com/template-category/iec-62366-templates/)). The window is perhaps 12–24 months [Estimate]. Reaching $1M ARR takes about **40–125 paying accounts** at $8k–25k ACV [Estimate]. That is a small slice of roughly 3,200 US 510(k) clearances a year and more than 38,000 European medtech companies. The binding constraints are founder hours, a 60–120-day regulated sales cycle, and buyer validation and security demands. Market size is not the problem. The largest personal risk sits before any of that: the founder's invention-assignment clause and duty of loyalty to a device-industry employer. It is also the cheapest risk to test, and the first one to test.

Throughout this report, a **sourced fact** carries an inline citation. Anything marked **[Estimate]** is my inference or modeling. Neither the research notes nor any public source verified it.

## Pros and cons at a glance

The case for the business rests on three things: a forcing regulatory event, a documented pain point (keeping documents consistent with each other), and the founder's domain credibility. The case against rests on part-time capacity, incumbents that could add the feature cheaply, and the trust and validation burden of selling into a QMS.

## A forced format change lands on a large base of HF files

The base volume is solid. openFDA data shows **3,198 510(k) clearances, 27 De Novo grants and 38 original PMAs in CY2025**. 510(k)s made up 98% of new authorizations ([Pure Global](https://www.pureglobal.com/blog-posts/fda-cleared-vs-approved-vs-granted-510k-pma-de-novo)), and a second dataset counts 3,195 ([510kdatabase.net](https://510kdatabase.net/clearances-2025)). Europe has **more than 38,000 medtech companies, about 90% of them SMEs** ([MedTech Europe](https://www.medtecheurope.org/datahub/introduction/)). Each of them needs a usability engineering file for every device under MDR, harmonised through EN 62366-1 ([VDE](https://www.vde.com/topics-en/health/consulting/usability-engineering-for-medical-devices-iec-62366)). Under FDA's tiered approach, every submission needs at least an HF summary or justification, but only some need a full validation report. FDA does not publish the share in each category. Assuming 30–50% of US submissions carry substantial HF documentation gives roughly **1,000–1,600 US HF files a year** [Estimate]. On top of that sits the EU stock of legacy files being moved to MDR. By document count, the EU is plausibly the larger market. By services spend, the US is [Estimate].

The money currently goes to people. Market-research firms size global HF and usability engineering services at about **USD 1.09B**, growing at about 7% a year ([Spherical Insights](https://sphericalinsights.com/reports/human-factors-and-usability-engineering-services-market); [Roots Analysis](https://www.rootsanalysis.com/press-releases/human-factors-and-usability-engineering-services-market-growth.html)). Treat those as low-confidence projections. One practitioner puts a formative testing program at **$60K–$120K** and a summative validation at "low six figures" ([Lana Hasher](https://www.linkedin.com/in/lrhasher)). In-house medtech HF engineers earn an average base of about **$85.5K** (San Jose about $103K) ([Payscale](https://payscale.com/research/US/Job=Human_Factors_Engineer/Salary/ffe667ff/Medical-Devices)). If documentation is 20–40% of consultant hours, a full moderate-risk US HF program costs $200–400K externally, and the documentation share a copilot could address is $40–160K per program [Estimate]. That comparison is the one that supports a $10–25K price. Comparing against template prices ($150–$500) does not support it.

The evidence of pain is real but mostly qualitative. **More than three-quarters of 510(k)s** get an additional-information request in the first review cycle ([RAPS](https://www.raps.org/resource/medical-device-performance-goals-fda-offers-a-qua.html)). A regulatory law blog says HF reviews generate "a good percentage" of burdensome requests ([The FDA Law Blog](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2023/02/the-new-fda-draft-human-factors-guidance-a-bridge-too-far/)). No public figure gives the HF share. The recurring failure modes are incomplete URRAs, missing critical-task identification, thin validation data, and usability files disconnected from the ISO 14971 risk file ([Rhizome AI](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/human-factors-deficiencies-in-fda-premarket); [TÜV NORD](https://www.tuv-nord.com/pl/en/uslugi/medical-devices/mdr-knowledge-hub/usability-engineering-of-non-active-medical-devices-in-light-of-the-mdr/)). These are documentation and traceability defects, which is exactly the class of problem software catches well. Use error accounts for **28.1%** of MAUDE reports that carry device-problem codes ([HFES/SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/ref/10.1177/2327857920091024)), which keeps HF on regulators' agendas. Several widely repeated claims could not be traced to FDA data and should stay out of any pitch deck: "nearly half of recalls stem from UI design" and "90% of HF reports are deficient."

Three newer changes raise the stakes. The QMSR took effect on **February 2, 2026**. It incorporates ISO 13485 and a new inspection program (CP 7382.850), and investigators can review pre-2026 records ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-advice-comprehensive-regulatory-assistance/quality-and-compliance-medical-devices)). FDA's own review AI, Elsa, has had agentic capabilities since December 2025 ([PDA](https://pda.org/pda-letter-portal/home/full-article/news-brief-fda-expands-ai-with-agentic-deployment)), and law firms now advise sponsors to make filings internally consistent because AI can screen them in minutes ([Fox Rothschild](https://www.foxrothschild.com/publications/fdas-ai-tool-can-now-review-your-submission-in-six-minutes-is-your-filing-ready)). When the regulator checks consistency by machine, a tool that checks consistency before submission becomes much easier to sell. Standards churn adds steady demand for template updates: IEC 62366-1 hits its 2026 stability date, TS 62366-2 is under revision, and a new TS 62366-3 on AI/ML usability is at CD stage ([IEC](https://webstore.iec.ch/publication/67220); [iss.rs tracker](https://iss.rs/en/project/show/iec:proj:131875)). One secondary source reports an updated "Applying HF/UE" guidance in August 2026 ([Intelacia](https://www.intelacia.com/2026/08/26/medical-device-usability-fda-human-factors-guidance)). That is unconfirmed on fda.gov.

## The gap is HF content, and it will close within about two years

Every incumbent treats usability as a sub-case of design controls plus risk management. They supply trace links and templates, and the HF engineer still writes the content. Aligned Elements ships a preconfigured IEC 62366-1 traceability setup ([Aligned Elements](https://www.aligned.ch/support-center/extensions/configurations-for-med-tech-standards/443-the-aligned-elements-iec-62366-configuration)). Jama's AI scores requirement quality ([Matrix One](https://matrixone.health/blog/requirements-management-tools-ai-features)). Greenlight Guru's Risk Intelligence suggests hazards and harms from adverse-event data, but its usability support is free templates ([Greenlight](https://greenlight.guru/risk-intelligence); [Greenlight use-spec template](https://www.greenlight.guru/downloads/use-specification-template)). Emergo by UL's **OPUS**, a freemium HFE platform launched in 2022, is the closest product analog. It offers guided process plus document libraries, with no evident generative AI ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/emergo-by-ul-launches-digital-human-factors-engineering-platform-for-medical-device-manufacturers-301499296.html)). Emergo's HF team did present on building custom AI tools for HFE at HFES 2025 ([Emergo](https://www.emergobyul.com/resources/emergo-ul-hfrd-team-highlights-2025-hfes-health-care-symposium)).

The most dangerous competitor is the cheapest one. Formwork lists IEC 62366 usability engineering inside an eQMS that costs **€99/month (Starter) to €499/month (QMS + Techdoc, €5,489/yr)** ([OpenRegulatory pricing](https://openregulatory.com/pricing); [Formwork pricing article](https://openregulatory.com/articles/formwork-eqms-pricing-and-when-you-should-upgrade)). It also publishes a free clause-to-document mapping for IEC 62366-1 ([OpenRegulatory mapping](https://openregulatory.com/document_templates/iec-62366-12015-mapping-of-requirements-to-documents)). A startup already on Formwork will not pay $15K a year for a usability add-on unless the add-on clearly does work that a template plus generic AI cannot. In practice that means checking consistency across documents and generating complete scenario coverage, with the ISO 14971 risk file in the loop.

The generalist AI regulatory-documentation startups are a second threat. Ketryx raised a **$39M Series B** in September 2025 for "validated AI agents" ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/09/04/ketryx-raises-39m-help-medical-device-makers-ship-safer-products-faster/)). Klaris raised about $1M pre-seed in February 2026 for technical-documentation consistency checks ([UKTN](https://www.uktech.news/medtech/klaris-medical-device-pre-seed-20260217)). FormlyAI raised a $2M seed ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/formlyai-2-million-raised-for-medical-device-certification-solution)). i-GENTIC launched 510(k) consistency agents whose showcase example is an intended-use change that never reached the risk analysis ([AdvaMed](https://www.advamed.org/industry-updates/news/i-gentic-ai-launches-context-aware-medtech-agents-to-strengthen-fda-510k-submission-consistency/)). HF is an obvious next module for any of them. The realistic window before an incumbent ships "draft my URRA / use specification" is **12–24 months** [Estimate]. A part-time founder cannot outbuild these players across all regulatory content. The defensible position is depth: HF-specific logic (use specification to task analysis to PCA analysis to hazard-related use scenarios to critical tasks to summative test cases to residual risk), native output in FDA's new report structure, and exports into Formwork, Greenlight, Jama and Matrix so the tool sits beside the eQMS instead of competing with it.

HF consultancies are both a channel and a risk. The market is consolidating: ClariMed bought UserWise (2023) and We Are Human (2025), and Core Human Factors is part of Rimkus ([PrivSource](https://www.privsource.com/acquisitions/deal/KLXSvN); [ONdrugDelivery](https://ondrugdelivery.com/?p=2135)). Firms without their own software have a margin incentive on fixed-fee projects to buy a productivity tool. A direct-to-manufacturer pitch built around "replace your consultant" would turn them into opponents [Estimate]. Customer complaint evidence is thin. No Reddit or forum threads on HF tooling were retrieved. Greenlight Guru reviews are positive (G2 4.5/5 across 411 reviews), with complaints about price and inflexibility rather than usability features ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/greenlight-guru-quality-management-software/reviews)). That gap is the strongest reason the first validation step should be interviews.

## Five risks are manageable, and the employment risk comes first

The research does not show a regulatory ban. It shows a validation and accuracy burden that the product design has to absorb. No regulator prohibits AI-drafted submissions, and no disclosure requirement for device submissions was found. Accountability stays wholly with the manufacturer. Notified bodies judge whether documentation shows *how* each GSPR is met, and "a simple collection of test/verification reports does not fulfil this requirement" ([Team-NB BPG v4](https://www.team-nb.org/team-nb-position-paper-on-bpg-for-the-submission-of-td-v4/)). Generic AI boilerplate is therefore a compliance risk in itself. The tool is also QMS software. Johner Institute argues that LLM systems used in QM processes fall under ISO 13485 4.1.6, with an exception arguable only when every output is fully verified ([Johner](https://blog.johner-institute.com/regulatory-affairs/validating-chatgpt/)). FDA's CSA guidance, re-issued on February 3, 2026 to align with the QMSR, allows lighter assurance in proportion to risk ([FDA](https://fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/computer-software-assurance-production-and-quality-management-system-software)).

Accuracy is the most important technical risk. In a URRA, the worst failure is **omission**: a hazardous use scenario that never appears. Fabrication is less dangerous because a reviewer is more likely to spot it. Retrieval-grounded legal AI tools still erred **17–34%** of the time ([Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-trial-legal-models-hallucinate-1-out-6-queries)). Court-filing hallucination incidents reportedly reached **1,981 by August 2026** ([Vaquill](https://www.vaquill.ai/blog/hallucination-tracker-charlotin-risk-manager)), and FDA's Elsa "confidently hallucinates" by its own staff's account ([Medical Economics](https://www.medicaleconomics.com/view/fda-s-new-ai-tool-elsa-faces-accuracy-concerns-despite-commissioner-s-high-hopes)). LLMs do best on divergent tasks such as brainstorming failure modes, and they produce generic, inconsistent guidance without expert review ([Risk Analysis](https://search.napstic.cn/literature/periodical/0220250700146519); [Design Science](https://resolve-he.cambridge.org/core/journals/design-science/article/aidriven-fmea-integration-of-large-language-models-for-faster-and-more-accurate-risk-analysis/22F110A2BF0DB4D01A69472CF17A0B43)). No peer-reviewed study of LLMs drafting IEC 62366-1 files exists, so accuracy in this exact domain is unknown and has to be measured.

On liability, the worst case is downstream. A missed use hazard contributes to patient harm, and the manufacturer seeks recovery from the vendor. Insurance Services Office (ISO) GenAI exclusion forms have seen **4,078 filed adoptions**, and some carriers apply absolute AI exclusions across E&O ([CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/CSA_research_note_ai_insurance_exclusion_wave_20260830-csa-styled.pdf); [Lathrop GPM](https://www.lathropgpm.com/insights/the-ai-coverage-gap-what-new-insurance-exclusions-mean-for-your-business/)). Specialist AI cover from Armilla and Corgi is emerging ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/big-insurance-backs-away-from-ai-risk-and-startups-rush-in/)). The EU's revised Product Liability Directive, which covers software and applies from December 2026, was not researched and needs legal review.

Employment risk is personal and comes first. Invention-assignment clauses routinely capture work that "relates to the employer's business or actual or demonstrably anticipated R&D." California §2870 protects only own-time inventions outside that exception, and the employee bears the burden of proof ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/2020/code-lab/division-3/chapter-2/article-3-5/section-2870/)). A usability-documentation tool built by an R&D PM at a device maker that does HF work sits squarely inside that exception [Estimate]. The FTC non-compete ban is dead, since the FTC dismissed its appeals in September 2025 ([Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/ftc-shifts-approach-to-non-compete-enforcement)). The duty of loyalty still lets an employee *prepare* a business but not *compete* while employed ([UBG Law](https://businessinsights.ubglaw.com/2021/02/employees-and-officers-what-duties-do-they-owe-to-their-employer-in-the-absence-of-a-contract/)). In Germany, post-employment non-competes require compensation of at least 50% of pay to be binding (§74 HGB), and statutory non-compete duties apply during employment ([Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/germany/nachvertragliche-wettbewerbsverbote-deutsche-regelungen-im-internationalen-kontext)). The consulting-first path raises this risk further: paid HF work for other manufacturers while employed is closer to "competing" than building software is [Estimate].

## The business plan: a consistency checker first, sold per device program

### Positioning

Lead with **"inspection- and review-ready usability files: every use scenario traced to the risk file and the summative protocol, in FDA's 2026 HF report structure."** Drafting is secondary. The documented pain is cross-document consistency, and this framing keeps the customer's engineer as author, which in turn lowers the validation burden. Pitch it to consultancies as "HF engineer leverage." Pitch it to manufacturers as "fewer AI letters and NB findings on HF, and less consultant time on paperwork." The product should stay out of recruiting, moderation and lab work; those remain services [Estimate].

### Pricing and packaging

All prices below are [Estimate]. They sit above Formwork's whole-eQMS price and well below consultant hours.

| Package | Buyer | Price | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Check** (self-serve) | Startups, solo HF consultants | $300–500/month per device (~$4–6K/yr) | Upload documents. Get a consistency and completeness report against IEC 62366-1 and the FDA 2026 structure |
| **Program** (core) | SMB/mid manufacturers | $10–15K/yr per active device program | Drafting of use spec, user profiles, task/PCA analysis, URRA linked to ISO 14971, formative/summative protocol and report skeletons, HF/UE report. Includes traceability matrix, validation pack and eQMS export |
| **Consultancy** | HF firms | $20–40K/yr (multi-project, multi-seat) | Program features across client projects, white-label exports, priority support |
| **File sprint** (services) | Any | $15–40K fixed fee | Founder-delivered AI-assisted HF file. Bridge revenue that converts to Program |

Per-device-program pricing is the best fit. It follows how HF files are scoped (one file per device), avoids seat objections at companies where only 1–3 people touch HF, and keeps the subscription alive through post-market changes [Estimate]. For anchors: Matrix Requirements lists €710–2,600+/month ([SoftwareFinder](https://softwarefinder.com/enterprise-resource-planning-software/matrix-requirements)), and Greenlight Guru users report $25–35K/yr as typical ([OpenRegulatory](https://openregulatory.com/articles/greenlight-guru-price)).

Against roughly 1,000–1,600 US HF files a year plus the EU base [Estimate], 88 device programs is a small share of the market. Capacity is the harder constraint. At an assumed 10–15% annual logo churn [Estimate], a part-time founder has to close about 25–30 new accounts a year near the end of the climb, through 60–120-day cycles that include QA supplier qualification under ISO 13485 §7.4 ([SimplerQMS](https://simplerqms.com/supplier-audit/)). The generic B2B median is 42 days for $10–100K deals ([The Starr Conspiracy](https://www.thestarrconspiracy.com/insights/benchmarks/b2b-go-to-market-strategy-benchmarks-2025)), and regulated buyers add qualification steps on top. That volume of founder-led selling and support is effectively a full-time job.

### Go-to-market channels

Content and templates are the documented playbook in this niche. OpenRegulatory grew on free templates, starter videos and competitor-pricing articles ([OpenRegulatory](https://openregulatory.com/articles/greenlight-guru-price)). Greenlight Guru paired a QA/RA founder with heavy content, though it was venture-funded with $2.25M early and $120M later ([Techpoint](https://techpoint.org/health-tech-startup-greenlight-guru-secures-1-25mm-launches-qms-software/)). In priority order [Estimate], the channels are:

1. A free lead magnet built around the new FDA HF report structure and eSTAR category selection, plus a URRA-to-summative traceability template.
2. LinkedIn writing aimed at HF/UE leads and RA/QA heads.
3. Design-partner deals with two or three mid-size HF consultancies (for example ClariMed/UserWise, Core HF or Agilis).
4. Talks rather than booths at the HFES Health Care Symposium.

Comparison pages against Formwork, OPUS and generic ChatGPT use are standard in the category ([Matrix One](https://matrixone.health/alternatives/formly-ai)). Conference costs and template conversion rates were not found.

### The consulting-first path, and its catch

Selling 3–6 fixed-fee "AI-assisted HF file" engagements funds development, produces case studies for supplier qualification, and tests willingness to pay [Estimate]. OpenRegulatory's hybrid services-plus-software model shows the pattern is viable ([OpenRegulatory](https://openregulatory.com/qms-software/formwork/)). No quantified services-to-software origin story was found. For this particular founder, consulting is also the activity most likely to breach duty-of-loyalty or conflict-of-interest terms while employed. It should start only after written employer approval and only for clients outside the employer's device categories. Cap it at about 30% of venture hours so it does not crowd out the product [Estimate].

## Verdict: feasible as a niche business; $1M depends on going full-time

The business is **feasible**. A real business plan exists: the demand driver is timely, the whitespace is identifiable, unit economics are strong, and the founder fits the market. As stated, though, **$1M ARR built part-time is a stretch target**. The most likely part-time outcome is a profitable $200–500K ARR niche product within about four years, plus optional services revenue. $1M becomes realistic once the founder commits full-time and adds one or two contractors [Estimate]. That fits the bootstrapped pattern: MicroConf finds most independent SaaS exits happen at $1–3M ARR, at 1–3× ARR ([MicroConf](https://microconf.com/saas-this-year)). The plan breaks if one of three things happens: the employment contract blocks it, measured accuracy shows the tool misses use hazards at rates experts will not tolerate, or Formwork or Greenlight ships a good-enough HF module before the product has references.

Each key assumption has a test that costs very little. The tests are ordered so that no money or code is spent before the decisive ones are passed.

| Key assumption | Cheapest test [Estimate] | Pass signal |
|---|---|---|
| The employer will not claim the IP or object | 1–2 hour employment-lawyer review (~$500–2K), then a written approval request | Written carve-out |
| Cross-document consistency is a paid pain, not just a nice-to-have | 20 discovery calls, asking about the last AI letter / NB finding on HF and hours spent reconciling URRA ↔ risk file ↔ protocol | ≥10 describe recent, costly incidents unprompted |
| Buyers will pay $10–15K per device program | Ask for 3 paid pilots or LOIs at $5K before building more | ≥2 signed |
| The LLM can draft and check without dangerous omissions | Build a golden set of 3 synthetic devices (e.g., autoinjector, infusion app, home-use monitor) with expert URRAs; measure recall of hazard-related use scenarios and consistency-flag precision | Recall ≥90% with human review; flags judged useful by 3 HF engineers |
| Consultancies will adopt, not resist | Demo to 3 mid-size HF firms; offer a free month on a live project | 1 paying partner within 6 months |
| The 2026 FDA structure is a working hook | Publish a free "new HF report structure / eSTAR category" template + checklist; measure sign-ups over 60 days | ≥200 qualified sign-ups and ≥5 sales conversations |
| Supplier qualification without SOC 2 is possible at SMBs | Ask 5 QA leads what they would require to qualify a drafting tool | ≥3 accept a questionnaire + validation pack |
| AI-output liability is insurable | Get 2–3 quotes for tech E&O with affirmative AI wording | Quote under ~$7K/yr at $1–2M limits |
| Incumbents are not about to ship | Check Formwork, Greenlight AI and OPUS release notes quarterly; ask design partners what their eQMS vendor promised | No HF drafting/check feature announced in the next two quarters |

## Conclusion

The opportunity is less "AI writes the usability file" than "AI proves the usability file holds together." FDA's August 2026 restructuring, QMSR inspections and FDA's own machine review all reward internal consistency. Consistency checking is also the framing that keeps a qualified human as author, which lightens tool validation and reduces liability. Drafting is the feature that gets attention. Traceability and completeness checking is what justifies a $10–15K price against a €499/month eQMS that already ships IEC 62366 templates.

Feasibility for this founder rests on time and legal clearance, more than on whether the market exists. The first two moves are a lawyer's read of the employment contract and an omission-rate benchmark on synthetic device files. Together they cost a few thousand dollars and a few weekends, and they settle the two questions that could end the venture before any customer matters. If both pass, three paid pilots within the first year will show whether this is a $300K side business or a $1M company worth quitting for.
