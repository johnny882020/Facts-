# Three AI Problems Companies Already Fund

Companies are paying most clearly to fix three problems: (1) governing and securing AI that employees and agents use without oversight, (2) customer-facing AI that fails people and offers no route to a human, and (3) the gap between AI pilots and measurable value, including the rework needed to correct AI output. Funding and M&A are largest for the first problem, where Veeam agreed to buy Securiti AI for about **$1.7B** and Cyera's reported valuation reached **$12B**. Funding for the second is also large, with Sierra reportedly at **$15.8B**, Parloa at **$3B** and Decagon at **$4.5B**. The third has the broadest surveys and real seat-based spend such as Microsoft 365 Copilot's reported **30M paid seats**, but it is the least cleanly priced as a standalone category. The evidence has a common weakness. Most figures come from vendor-run surveys, analyst forecasts, trade-press funding reports or aggregators, and the research behind this report could not open Gartner or IBM primary pages. Funding and valuations show that investors expect buyers to pay. They are not buyer spend, and almost no contract values or ARR figures are public. Read every number below as "reported by", and treat the problems and the direction of demand as better supported than any single statistic.

## Unsupervised AI use and agents are the best-funded problem

The evidence of the problem starts with IBM's 2025 Cost of a Data Breach report. Secondary coverage says organizations with high shadow-AI use paid about **$670,000 more per breach**, and roughly **1 in 5** organizations reported a breach tied to shadow AI. The same coverage says only about **37%** have policies to manage AI or detect shadow AI ([Technology Magazine](https://technologymagazine.com/news/ibms-cost-of-a-data-breach-report-on-risks-of-ai-breaches); [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-ai-breach-risks-rise-as-governance-lags/)). Gartner forecasts that more than **40%** of organizations will suffer shadow-AI security or compliance incidents by 2030 ([Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/gartner-40-firms-hit-shadow-ai/)). Agent oversight looks thinner still. Gartner reports only **13%** of IT application leaders have appropriate governance structures for AI agents ([IDM](https://www.idm.net.au/node/15402)). A Cloud Security Alliance survey found **82%** of enterprises have unknown AI agents in their environments ([The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/fraud-and-fincrime/news/csa-survey-finds-82-of-enterprises-have-unknown-ai-agents-in-their-environments)). AvePoint's survey of 750 leaders put data leakage (**50.1%**) and manipulation through malicious inputs, essentially prompt injection (**49.6%**), at the top of reported incident types ([Help Net Security](https://helpnetsecurity.com/2026/06/11/owasp-prompt-injection-ai-security-failures)). Gravitee reports 88% of executives saw an agent security incident while 82% believed their policies protected them, and Arkose Labs reports only 6% of security budgets go to agentic AI risk ([NeuralTrust](https://neuraltrust.ai/guides/the-state-of-ai-agent-security-2026)). That mismatch of high incidence, high confidence and low budget is the clearest sign of demand that has not yet been funded.

Willingness to pay shows up in capital markets. Veeam announced the roughly **$1.7B** acquisition of data-security-posture vendor Securiti AI in October 2025 ([CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/veeam-securiti-ai-acquisition/)). Cyera's valuation reportedly rose from $3B in November 2024 to **$12B** on a $600M round, with more than $2B raised in total ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/cyera-raises-600-million-at-12-billion-valuation/)). Agent-specific vendors raised large rounds in 2026, including Onyx Security's $113M Series B, HiddenLayer's $100M Series B and Noma Security's $132M growth round ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/topics/onyx-security/); [Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/ai/news/12-agentic-ai-startups-to-watch-in-2026-b9e1556f-8947-4c7c-80ab-1a88e62767e4)). SentinelOne bought Prompt Security in August 2025, and one outlet reported $250-300M ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/topics/acquires/)). Evaluation and monitoring platforms are funded at smaller scale, such as Braintrust's $80M Series B and Arize's $70M Series C ([Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/ai/deals/braintrust-raises-80m-series-b-to-become-ai-observability-layer-6595f521-47a8-4413-bf58-77adeb77d694/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/20/arize-ai-hopes-it-has-first-mover-advantage-in-ai-observability)). Regulation adds a compliance push. The EU Digital Omnibus reportedly moves high-risk obligations to December 2027, and a secondary citation of Bruegel puts compliance at EUR 14,623-29,277 per system ([JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/eu-legislators-agree-to-delay-for-high-5617765/); [Verdantix](https://www.verdantix.com/client-portal/blog/the-eu-ai-act-omnibus--quiet-changes-and-hidden-risks)).

The caveats are substantial. The primary IBM and Gartner documents were not read. Baseline breach costs conflict between secondary sources ($4.63M vs $4.74M), and only the $670K premium matched. The AvePoint, Gravitee and Arkose figures come from vendors with a product to sell, and some sources attribute the 88% figure to different surveys. The M&A evidence is partial, since Google-Wiz ($32B) is broad cloud security and the Protect AI price and buyer conflict across sources. The delay and legal status of the EU rules also conflict across sources. No named, dollar-quantified prompt-injection breach, no contract values and no budget-share survey were found.

## Customer-facing AI fails people who cannot reach a human

The problem is documented from the customer side. Gartner's 2024 survey of 5,728 customers found **64%** would prefer companies not use AI for customer service, and the top concern, at **60%**, was difficulty reaching a human ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-07-09-gartner-survey-finds-64-percent-of-customers-would-prefer-that-companies-didnt-use-ai-for-customer-service)). Its 2026 survey of 3,566 customers reports **87%** say a human must be available, although 50% say GenAI makes interactions easier ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-08-04-gartner-survey-finds-87-percent-of-customers-say-companies-using-genai-for-customer-service-must-provide-access-to-a-human-agent0)). Qualtrics, surveying more than 20,000 consumers, found nearly 1 in 5 who used AI for customer service saw no benefit, about **four times** the failure rate of AI use generally ([Qualtrics](https://www.qualtrics.com/articles/news/ai-powered-customer-service-fails-at-four-times-the-rate-of-other-tasks/)). Forrester predicts a third of brands will erode customer trust through premature self-service AI ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251028326446/en/Forrester%E2%80%99s-2026-B2C-Marketing-CX-Digital-Business-Predictions-One-Third-Of-Brands-Will-Erode-Customer-Trust-Through-Self-Service-AI)). Named incidents show the buyer-side cost. A tribunal held Air Canada liable for its chatbot's wrong fare advice, rejecting the claim that the bot was a separate legal entity, though the award was only C$812.02 ([American Bar Association](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2024-february/bc-tribunal-confirms-companies-remain-liable-information-provided-ai-chatbot/)). Klarna's CEO said cost-focused automation lowered quality and the company resumed hiring human agents ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/klarna-ceo-reverses-course-by-hiring-more-humans-not-ai/491396)). Commonwealth Bank of Australia reversed 45 planned job cuts after admitting its assessment was wrong ([Information Age](https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2025/cba-reverses-ai-driven-job-cuts--admits--error-.html)). Taco Bell scaled back voice-AI drive-through tests after viral errors ([Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2025/ai/taco-bell-scales-back-ai-tests-after-customer-complaints)).

The direct dollar losses are small, so the paying motive is the cost of getting service wrong at scale: churn, rehiring, brand damage and liability. Money has followed. Sierra's raise was reported at **$950M at $15.8B** in 2026, after $175M at $4.5B in 2024, and its co-founder sizes customer service at about $400B a year ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/investment-tracker/2026/openai-veteran-raises-950-million-for-ai-customer-service-agents/); [CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/customer-experience/sierra-raises-950m-at-15b-valuation-eyes-transformation-beyond-customer-support/)). Decagon raised $250M at $4.5B in January 2026, tripling its valuation in seven months ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-28/ai-customer-support-startup-decagon-valued-at-4-5-billion)). Parloa raised $350M at $3B, reports ARR above **$50M** and names Allianz, Booking.com and SAP as customers ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/01/15/parloa-raises-350m-tripling-valuation-to-3bn/)). ElevenLabs raised $500M at $11B ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/series-d)). Cresta, which sells a unified platform for human and virtual agents, raised $125M in 2024 ([Cresta](https://www.cresta.com/blog/cresta-raises-125m-to-create-the-unified-platform-for-human-and-virtual-agents)). Gartner reports **91%** of service leaders face pressure to implement AI in 2026, though this is a press-release title only ([Gartner](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-02-18-gartner-survey-finds-ninety-one-percent-of-customer-service-leaders-under-pressure-to-implement-ai-in-2026)). Salesforce says Agentforce handles about half of its support conversations after support headcount fell from about 9,000 to 5,000, a self-reported claim ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/09/02/salesforce_4000_jobs_ai)).

The caveats cut both ways. Buyer demand is partly top-down pressure to adopt, not a diagnosed failure being repaired, and the investor signal is not buyer spend. Valuations conflict across trackers (Sierra $10B vs $15.8B vs an $18.56B tracker estimate; ElevenLabs $11B, $17.6B and $22B), and no contract sizes, per-resolution prices or earnings-call data were found. The survey data come from Gartner press releases seen only through search summaries, and the question wording is unseen. A 27% "would retry a chatbot" figure is unverified. The proposed California and New York human-access rules came from low-quality sources. The market is also splitting, with a "replace agents" pitch alongside a hybrid human-plus-AI pitch, which fits the Klarna and CBA reversals.

## Pilots that fail to pay back, and rework that erodes gains

The third problem is the gap between AI adoption and measurable value. MIT NANDA reports about **95%** of enterprise GenAI pilots show no measurable P&L impact, blaming integration and a learning gap over model quality ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/)). McKinsey finds only **39%** report any EBIT impact, and about 6% are high performers ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai-2025)). BCG finds about 60% report minimal gains and only about 5% are "future-built" ([BCG](https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2025-ai-leaders-outpace-laggards-revenue-growth-cost-savings)). Deloitte reports only 25% have moved 40% or more of pilots to production and 84% have not redesigned roles ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/state-of-ai-report-2026)). S&P Global reports 42% of companies abandoned most AI initiatives ([CIO Dive](https://www.ciodive.com/news/ai-project-fail-data-spglobal/742590/)). Gartner predicts over 40% of agentic projects will be canceled by 2027, and 60% of projects without AI-ready data will be abandoned, both forecasts ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027); [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-02-26-lack-of-ai-ready-data-puts-ai-projects-at-risk)). Workday adds a mechanism, finding about **37%** of time saved by AI is lost to rework and only 14% consistently get net-positive outcomes ([HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/ai-output-reduced-rework-low-quality-workday/810075/)). METR's trial of 16 experienced developers found AI-assisted work took 19% longer while developers believed they were about 20% faster ([IT Pro](https://www.itpro.com/software/development/think-ai-coding-tools-are-speeding-up-work-think-again-theyre-actually-slowing-developers-down)). Worker-side data point to skills and trust gaps: PwC finds only half of workers can get the training they need ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/pwc-survey-workers-ai-engine-room-training)), and ManpowerGroup finds AI use rising 13% while confidence fell 18% ([Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2026/01/21/ai-workers-toxic-relationship-trust-confidence-collapses-training-manpower-group)). Deloitte Australia's partial refund of an A$440,000 report with fabricated citations is the best-known dollar case ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/10/07/deloitte-ai-australia-government-report-hallucinations-technology-290000-refund)).

Willingness to pay here shows up as spending on tools sold as solutions, with an important finding that buyers favor vendors: MIT reports bought tools succeed about two-thirds of the time against one-third for internal builds ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/)). Microsoft 365 Copilot reached a reported **30M paid seats** by late July 2026 ([UC Today](https://uctoday.com/microsoft-365-copilot-passes-30-million-paid-seats-as-cloud-and-ai-growth-power-record-quarter)), which at the $30 list price implies about $7.2B annualized (third-party arithmetic), yet only an estimated 6.6% of commercial seats ([No Jitter](https://www.nojitter.com/digital-workplace/microsoft-365-copilot-adoption-jumps-50-percent-over-prior-quarter)). Salesforce reported Agentforce ARR of about **$540M**, up 330%, with 9,500+ paid deals ([Constellation Research](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/salesforce-delivers-strong-q3-agentforce-data-360-surge)). Glean reportedly reached about $300M ARR (aggregator source) ([KuCoin flash](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/glean-hits-300m-arr-amid-ai-budget-cuts-valued-at-7-2b)). Evaluation and observability funding (Braintrust, Arize) addresses the reliability slice directly.

This is the weakest of the three for pricing the problem itself. The headline numbers use different definitions (no P&L impact, abandoned, forecast cancellation) and cannot be added. The MIT result rests on 150 interviews, a 350-person survey and 300 public deployments. The Gartner figures are predictions. The Workday study carries a wording correction (37% of time saved, not of gains), and the Deloitte and Stanford figures came from summaries. No reliable aggregate dollar cost of failed pilots exists, and unverified blog figures ($7.2M per failed project) were excluded. The research found no revenue for a standalone verification layer, and training demand rests on mostly consultancy- or vendor-run surveys with no named contracts. The spend that exists is for broad products whose buyers have many motives.

## Conclusion

The three problems share a structure. In each, the reported pain is a gap between adoption and control: AI is deployed faster than it is governed, handed off to humans, or converted into measurable value. The strongest funded demand sits at the two ends, security and governance of AI and agents, and the customer-service agents that sell human-plus-AI fixes. The third problem, value and reliability, is the most documented in surveys but the least separately priced. Treat it as the demand that supports the other two. The hybrid pivot at Klarna, CBA and Cresta suggests buyers are paying to correct over-automation, not only to automate.

The main unresolved question is whether this money reflects buyer budgets or investor expectation. No source gives contract values, budget shares for oversight, or per-incident losses large enough to justify these valuations on their own. The most valuable follow-up is primary verification: IBM and Gartner documents, vendor earnings calls, and procurement or budget surveys.
